Glossary

Plain-language definitions of terms used across the guides and research.

Absolute risk · ADHD · Alexithymia · Allopregnanolone · AuDHD · Autism · Autistic burnout · Autistic inertia · BFRB · C-PASS · Camouflaging / masking · Chronotype · Cohort study · Confidence interval (CI) · Diagnosis, community term or identity · Drospirenone pill · DRSP · Emotional dysregulation · Executive function · Ferritin · Follicular phase · GnRH agonist · Hazard ratio (HR) · hEDS / HSD · Hyperfocus · Interoception · Luteal phase · MCAS · Meta-analysis · Odds ratio (OR) · PDA · PMDD · PME · PMS · POTS · RCT · Registry study · Rejection sensitivity · Risk ratio (RR) · Sensory over-responsivity · SSRI · Statistically significant / no clear association · Twice-exceptional (2e)

Absolute risk
The actual chance of an outcome in a group, for example 2 in 100. Ratios (odds, risk, hazard) describe relative differences and can sound large when the absolute risk is small.
ADHD
Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder. A neurodevelopmental condition affecting attention, activity and impulse control, present from childhood. In women it is often recognised late. Read more →
Alexithymia
Difficulty identifying and describing your own emotions. Common in autism but not universal. Read more →
Allopregnanolone
A substance the body makes from progesterone. It acts on GABA-A receptors in the brain and is studied as one mechanism behind PMDD. Read more →
AuDHD
An informal community term for being both autistic and having ADHD. Not a separate diagnosis. Read more →
Autism
A neurodevelopmental condition involving differences in social communication, sensory processing and patterns of interest and behaviour. Read more →
Autistic burnout
Long-lasting exhaustion, loss of skills and lower tolerance for sensory input, described by autistic adults after prolonged strain. Not a formal diagnosis. Read more →
Autistic inertia
Difficulty starting, stopping or switching activities, described by autistic people. Barely studied. Read more →
BFRB
Body-focused repetitive behaviour, such as nail biting, skin picking or hair pulling. Read more →
C-PASS
A standard method for scoring two or more cycles of daily symptom ratings to diagnose PMDD. Read more →
Camouflaging / masking
Hiding or compensating for neurodivergent traits to fit in, often at a cost to energy and wellbeing. Read more →
Chronotype
Your natural body-clock timing: earlier (morning) or later (evening). Read more →
Cohort study
A study that follows a group of people over time to see who develops an outcome.
Confidence interval (CI)
A range showing how precise an estimate is. A narrow interval means the study pins the value down well; a wide one means more uncertainty. If a ratio's interval includes 1, the study did not show a clear difference, which is not the same as showing there is no difference.
Diagnosis, community term or identity
A diagnosis (such as ADHD, autism or PMDD) is defined by clinical criteria. A community term (such as AuDHD) describes a real experience or combination without being a separate diagnosis. An identity is how a person describes themselves. All three can be valid at once. Read more →
Drospirenone pill
A combined contraceptive pill (for example Yaz) studied as a treatment for PMDD. Read more →
DRSP
Daily Record of Severity of Problems. A daily symptom chart used to track PMDD across the cycle. Read more →
Emotional dysregulation
Emotions that come fast, feel intense and take a long time to settle. A recognised part of adult ADHD. Read more →
Executive function
The mental skills used to plan, start, organise, switch and finish tasks. Read more →
Ferritin
A blood test that reflects how much iron the body has stored. It can be low even when haemoglobin is normal. Read more →
Follicular phase
The first half of the menstrual cycle, from the start of a period to ovulation. Read more →
GnRH agonist
A medicine that temporarily switches off the ovarian cycle. Used for severe PMDD, usually with hormone 'add-back'. Read more →
Hazard ratio (HR)
Compares how quickly an outcome happens over time in one group versus another, in studies that follow people for different lengths of time. It is close to, but not the same as, a risk ratio.
hEDS / HSD
Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and hypermobility spectrum disorder: conditions involving very flexible joints and related symptoms. Read more →
Hyperfocus
Intense, absorbed concentration on something interesting, often losing track of time.
Interoception
Sensing signals from inside your body, such as hunger, thirst, heartbeat or needing the toilet. Read more →
Luteal phase
The second half of the menstrual cycle, after ovulation and before the period. PMDD symptoms occur here. Read more →
MCAS
Mast cell activation syndrome. Consensus criteria require typical symptoms in more than one organ system, a documented rise in mast-cell markers during symptoms, and a response to treatments that block mast-cell mediators. Popular claims linking MCAS to ADHD or autism have not been shown in research. Read more →
Meta-analysis
A study that combines the results of many studies to get a more reliable overall answer.
Odds ratio (OR)
Compares the odds of an outcome in one group with the odds in another. Odds are not the same as chances: if 20 in 100 people have something, the chance is 20% but the odds are 20 to 80 (0.25). When an outcome is common, an odds ratio can look much bigger than the real difference in risk, so it should not be read as 'times more likely'.
PDA
Pathological (or persistent) demand avoidance. A proposed autism profile involving avoidance of everyday demands; its validity is debated.
PMDD
Premenstrual dysphoric disorder: severe mood symptoms in the days before a period that ease after it starts. Read more →
PME
Premenstrual exacerbation: a condition present all month, such as ADHD or depression, gets worse before a period. Different from PMDD. Read more →
PMS
Premenstrual syndrome: physical and emotional symptoms in the days before a period that cause some distress or interference but do not meet the stricter criteria for PMDD. Read more →
POTS
Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome: a sustained heart-rate rise of at least 30 beats per minute (40 in people aged 12–19) within 10 minutes of standing, without a significant blood-pressure drop, with symptoms such as dizziness and fatigue for at least 3 months, after other causes have been ruled out. A wearable heart-rate reading alone cannot diagnose it. Read more →
RCT
Randomised controlled trial: people are assigned at random to a treatment or a comparison, the strongest test of whether something works.
Registry study
A study using national health records, often covering millions of people.
Rejection sensitivity
Anxiously expecting, quickly noticing and strongly reacting to rejection. 'RSD' is a popular term that has not been validated in research. Read more →
Risk ratio (RR)
Compares the chance (risk) of an outcome in one group with the chance in another. Example: if 4 in 100 people in one group and 2 in 100 in another develop something, the risk ratio is 2, but the absolute difference is only 2 in 100. Always look for the starting (baseline) risk as well.
Sensory over-responsivity
Finding ordinary sounds, lights, textures or smells overwhelming. Read more →
SSRI
Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, a type of antidepressant. A first-line treatment for PMDD, taken daily or only in the luteal phase. Read more →
Statistically significant / no clear association
A 'significant' result is unlikely to be due to chance alone under the study's assumptions; it says nothing about how large or important the effect is. 'No clear association' means the study could not show a difference, not that it proved there is none.
Twice-exceptional (2e)
Being both gifted and neurodivergent, so that each can hide the other. Read more →

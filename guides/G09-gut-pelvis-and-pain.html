Gut, pelvis & pain: IBS, endometriosis and ADHD

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short

  • People with ADHD have more IBS and constipation than people without ADHD Blake 2026 [S]. This is a link, not proof that ADHD causes gut problems.

  • Endometriosis often takes years to diagnose, and it is often labelled as IBS first Hudelist 2012 [S]; Seaman 2008 [S]. Ongoing pelvic and bowel pain deserves a gynaecological look.

  • One large Swedish national register study found ADHD and endometriosis linked in both directions Gao 2020 [P]. That's a single, early finding about an association, not proof that one causes the other. The "ADHD gut microbiome" and probiotics for ADHD are not supported Bundgaard-Nielsen 2020 [U].

Evidence grade: SUPPORTED (S) for ADHD and functional gut symptoms, and for the endometriosis–IBS overlap. PRELIMINARY (P) for ADHD and endometriosis, and for chronic pain. POPULAR BUT UNSUPPORTED (U) for an ADHD-specific microbiome and probiotics as ADHD treatment. Talk to a clinician about: pelvic pain that keeps coming back, painful or heavy periods, and gut symptoms that follow your cycle. Not medical advice.

If you are thinking about suicide or are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a local crisis line now.

1. ADHD and functional gut symptoms

"Functional" gut problems are ones where the gut works differently, but tests don't show damage. Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and chronic constipation are examples. "Organic" gut diseases, such as Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis or coeliac disease, do show up on tests.

What the research shows

  • A meta-analysis of 23 studies (more than 1.9 million people, all ages) found ADHD associated with more gut symptoms overall (OR 1.48), IBS (OR 1.58), constipation (OR 1.97) and soiling (encopresis, OR 4.32) Blake 2026 [S].
  • An odds ratio (OR) compares how likely something is in one group vs another. An OR above 1 means higher odds in the ADHD group. The bigger the number, the stronger the link.
  • This review left out studies of diagnosed organic gut disease. So it can't tell us whether those are more common in ADHD.
  • In 33,380 young adults with ADHD and 355,652 without (Israeli military recruits aged 17–35), ADHD went with more IBS (OR 1.67), chronic constipation (OR 1.64) and indigestion (OR 1.48). It did not go with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) or coeliac disease. The ADHD group was also referred to gut specialists more often (RR 1.96) Kedem 2020 [S].
  • In 742,939 children, constipation (4.1% vs 1.5%) and faecal incontinence (0.9% vs 0.15%) were more common with ADHD. Stimulant treatment showed no clear change in visit rates for these problems McKeown 2013 [S].

A complication for women. In Norwegian registers (2,500,118 people), women with ADHD had higher odds of Crohn's disease (1.44) and ulcerative colitis (1.28). Men with ADHD had lower odds of Crohn's Hegvik 2018 [P]. So "ADHD is linked to functional gut problems but not to gut disease" may be too simple for women. This is one study and needs repeating.

The cycle matters too.

  • In 156 healthy women, 73% had at least one gut symptom in the 5 days before or during their period. Abdominal pain and diarrhoea were the most common. Women with emotional symptoms were more likely to have several gut symptoms, and fatigue was common and tied to gut symptoms Bernstein 2014 [P].
  • In women with IBS who kept a daily diary over one cycle, gut and whole-body symptoms varied with the menstrual cycle Heitkemper 2003 [S].

2. Endometriosis: years of delay, and misdiagnosis as IBS

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the womb lining grows outside the womb. It affects about 5–10% of women of reproductive age, around 176 million people Zondervan 2018 [E]. It involves altered immunity and inflammation Zondervan 2018 [E].

The wait for a diagnosis

  • Across 1,418 women in 10 countries, the average delay from first symptoms to surgical diagnosis was 6.7 years. Most of the delay happened in primary care. The delay was 8.3 years in mainly state-funded health systems vs 5.5 years in others. Women with endometriosis lost an average of 10.8 hours of work a week, mainly through reduced effectiveness while working Nnoaham 2011 [S].
  • In 171 women in Austria and Germany, the median delay was 10.4 years. 74% received at least one false diagnosis first. Misdiagnosis, and women treating period pain as normal, both prolonged the delay Hudelist 2012 [S].
  • A major review notes that diagnosis is typically delayed by years and misdiagnosis is common Taylor 2021 [S].

IBS: a common wrong turn, and a real overlap

  • In a UK case-control study, women with endometriosis had higher odds of having received an IBS diagnosis (odds ratio, OR 3.5). Their odds of a new IBS diagnosis were still raised after endometriosis was confirmed (OR 2.5) Seaman 2008 [S].
  • Two meta-analyses found IBS more common in endometriosis: summary RR 2.39 Saidi 2020 [S], and OR 3.26 Chiaffarino 2021 [S].
  • The Saidi authors question whether standard IBS criteria can tell IBS apart from the gut sensitivity of endometriosis Saidi 2020 [S].
  • In the Estonian Biobank, 9.0% of women with IBS had endometriosis, and 13.6% of women with endometriosis had IBS Peters 2022 [S].

Each condition also had its own broad pattern of other health problems, partly distinct from the other's Peters 2022 [S].

So both things are true. Endometriosis can be mislabelled as IBS. And the two can genuinely occur together.

Diagnosis is changing

  • The European (ESHRE) guideline challenges keyhole surgery (laparoscopy) as the only "gold standard" test Becker 2022 [E].
  • Some endometriosis, such as ovarian cysts (endometriomas) and deep nodules, can be seen on ultrasound and MRI Zondervan 2018 [E].
  • Experts have argued for diagnosis from symptoms and examination, to reduce a delay of 4–11 years Agarwal 2019 [S].

3. Symptoms to name

In a UK study of 5,540 women with endometriosis and matched controls, those with endometriosis had more Ballard 2008 [S]:

  • abdominal and pelvic pain (OR 5.2)
  • painful periods (OR 8.1)
  • heavy periods (OR 4.0)
  • IBS diagnoses (OR 1.6)

Endometriosis may coexist with, or be misdiagnosed as, IBS or pelvic inflammatory disease Ballard 2008 [S].

NICE advises clinicians to suspect endometriosis when someone has one or more of: chronic pelvic pain; period pain that affects daily activities and quality of life; deep pain during or after sex; period-related or cyclical gut symptoms, especially painful bowel movements; period-related or cyclical urinary symptoms, especially blood in the urine or pain passing urine; or infertility together with any of these NICE NG73 [Guideline]. We did not find a checked research paper on how accurately each of these symptoms points to endometriosis in our search (last updated October 2026).

Practical tip. Write down every kind of pain you have, including ones that feel awkward to say out loud. Note when in your cycle it happens and how much it stops you doing things. "I miss two days of work every month" is clearer than "bad cramps".

4. ADHD × endometriosis: one Swedish register study, in both directions

  • Design and population. A national cohort study using Swedish health registers, with a sibling comparison (173,650 families) Gao 2020 [P].
  • Finding. Women with endometriosis later had higher risks of depression, anxiety, substance use and ADHD. The sibling-adjusted hazard ratio for ADHD was up to 1.98. Women with ADHD were also more likely to be diagnosed with endometriosis later Gao 2020 [P].
  • A hazard ratio (HR) compares how quickly something happens over time in two groups. It is a different measure from an odds ratio, and the two shouldn't be combined.
  • "Sibling-adjusted" means comparing sisters, which partly controls for shared family background.
  • A register study shows an association. It can't show that endometriosis causes ADHD or the reverse.
  • In UK Biobank (8,276 women with endometriosis), endometriosis was linked to depression (OR 3.61), eating disorders (2.94) and anxiety (2.61). Genetic analyses suggested some shared biology for depression and anxiety Koller 2023 [S]. That study did not look at ADHD.

We grade the ADHD–endometriosis link PRELIMINARY because it rests on a single register study, and why it happens is unknown. It should not be described as solid or established anywhere on this site.

5. Chronic pain in women with ADHD or autism

The evidence here is small and early.

  • 77 women diagnosed with ADHD and/or autism in childhood were followed for 16–19 years. As adults, 76.6% reported chronic pain. Those taking stimulant medication had less widespread pain Asztély 2019 [P].
  • Of 123 people with fibromyalgia (a long-term widespread pain condition), 44.72% screened positive for possible adult ADHD on a questionnaire van Rensburg 2018 [P]. A screen is not a diagnosis.
  • Genetic studies found strong overlap between ADHD and chronic pain in many body sites. The analyses suggested pain liability affects ADHD more than the reverse Ciochetti 2025 [P].
  • In 109 neurodivergent adults, very flexible joints (hypermobility) were common. The number of hypermobile joints helped explain links with pain Csecs 2022 [P]. See our guide G12 for more on hypermobility.

6. Claim check: "the ADHD gut microbiome" and probiotics

Your gut microbiome is the community of bacteria living in your gut.

  • Reviews describe ADHD microbiome findings as highly varied, with no clear consistent differences Bundgaard-Nielsen 2020 [P], or as inconclusive overall Gkougka 2022 [P].
  • Combining 4 adult cohorts (312 adults with ADHD, 305 controls), there were some differences, but they varied a lot between cohorts Jakobi 2024 [P].
  • Across psychiatric conditions, microbiome differences were mostly shared rather than specific to one condition. The shared pattern was reported for depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis and anxiety, not specifically ADHD Nikolova 2021 [P].
  • One small study (19 people with ADHD, 77 controls) linked certain gut bacteria to brain reward responses Aarts 2017 [P]. Interesting, but tiny.

We did not find a probiotic trial in ADHD that met our selection rules (search last updated October 2026). The claim that probiotics treat ADHD is therefore POPULAR BUT UNSUPPORTED [U]: not shown, rather than shown not to work.

7. What to ask your GP or gynaecologist

Before you go: what the research says about treatment.

  • A major review argues endometriosis is a whole-body (systemic) condition, not only a pelvic one. It describes inflammation, changes in metabolism, and brain changes that increase pain sensitivity and affect mood Taylor 2021 [S].
  • The same review notes that progestin hormones and low-dose contraceptive pills fail in a third of women with symptoms. Newer oral medicines (GnRH antagonists) are an alternative Taylor 2021 [S].
  • The European guideline says evidence was insufficient for firm recommendations on the most appropriate treatments Becker 2022 [E].

Practical tip. If a first treatment doesn't help, you are not alone, and it is reasonable to go back. NICE advises referral to a gynaecology service if initial treatment isn't effective, isn't tolerated or isn't suitable, or if symptoms affect daily life or keep coming back NICE NG73 [Guideline].

Practical tip. Phrases that may help:

  • "My pelvic pain has been going on for years and it stops me . Could this be endometriosis?"
  • "I was diagnosed with IBS, but my symptoms get worse around my period. Could something gynaecological be going on as well?"
  • "Is an ultrasound or MRI worth doing, or a referral to a gynaecologist?"
  • "My periods are heavy. Could you check my iron?" (See guide G06.)

When to ask for more help

Get an urgent GP appointment or contact NHS 111 (or your local equivalent) if you have NHS: IBS symptoms [Reference]:

  • weight loss you can't explain
  • bleeding from your bottom, or bloody diarrhoea
  • a hard lump or swelling in your tummy
  • breathlessness, a pounding heart (palpitations) and paler skin than usual

These aren't typical of IBS and need checking.

Ask your GP about endometriosis, and about referral, if you have NICE NG73 [Guideline]:

  • pelvic pain that is long-lasting, or period pain that affects work, study or daily life
  • deep pain during or after sex
  • bowel symptoms that follow your cycle, especially painful bowel movements
  • urinary symptoms that follow your cycle, especially blood in your urine or pain passing urine
  • any of the above together with difficulty getting pregnant
  • symptoms that continue despite first treatments such as painkillers or hormonal treatment

NICE also asks clinicians to remember that pain can be expressed differently, including by neurodivergent people NICE NG73 [Guideline]. If you find it hard to describe pain, a written symptom diary can help: NICE recommends one for these discussions NICE NG73 [Guideline].

What you can do

  1. Track gut symptoms, pelvic pain and bleeding against your cycle for two or three months.
  2. Write down every type of pain, when it happens, and what it stops you doing.
  3. If you have an IBS diagnosis but symptoms follow your cycle, ask whether endometriosis has been considered.
  4. Bring your ADHD or autism diagnosis (or suspicion) into the conversation. It is useful context, not a reason to dismiss pain.
  5. Be wary of anyone selling microbiome tests or probiotics as ADHD treatment.

Talk to a clinician about…

  • Pelvic pain, painful periods or heavy periods, especially if they disrupt work or study.
  • Gut symptoms that change with your cycle.
  • Whether imaging or a gynaecology referral makes sense for you.
  • Chronic pain, and whether your ADHD treatment plan takes it into account.
  • Low mood or anxiety alongside pain. Endometriosis is linked to both Koller 2023 [S].

What we don't know yet

  • How well individual symptoms point to endometriosis. NICE lists them as reasons to suspect endometriosis, but we did not find a checked research paper on how accurate each one is.
  • Adult women with ADHD and IBS. We did not find a meta-analysis specific to adult women.
  • Organic gut disease in women with ADHD. One Norwegian study found Crohn's and ulcerative colitis linked to ADHD in women only. It needs repeating.
  • Why ADHD and endometriosis are linked, and whether the link holds outside Sweden. It rests on a single register study.
  • Chronic pain. We did not find a high-quality meta-analysis of ADHD and chronic pain. The women's follow-up study had 77 people.
  • Probiotics. We did not find a probiotic trial in ADHD that met our selection rules.

(All "we did not find" statements refer to our search, last updated October 2026.) - Whether treating ADHD changes gut symptoms in adults. In children, stimulant treatment showed no clear change in visit rates for constipation McKeown 2013 [S].

Popular claims checked

  • adhd-and-ibs-go-together: "ADHD and IBS go together." Verdict: mostly true. Note: the Blake 2026 meta-analysis excluded organic gut disease rather than testing it, so it doesn't show "no link" with gut disease.
  • endometriosis-misdiagnosed-as-ibs: "Endometriosis often gets misdiagnosed as IBS." Verdict: mostly true. The two also genuinely overlap.
  • endometriosis-and-adhd-are-linked: "Endometriosis and ADHD are linked." Verdict: partly true, preliminary. A single Swedish national register study found an association in both directions; it doesn't show cause.
  • gut-microbiome-causes-adhd: "ADHD is caused by gut bacteria, and probiotics can treat it." Verdict: mostly true.

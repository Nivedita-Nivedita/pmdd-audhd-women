  • PMDD treatments ranked by evidence

    SSRIs have the strongest evidence; both continuous and luteal-phase dosing help, and the 2024 Cochrane review favours continuous dosing on average. Drospirenone pills offer modest benefit. GnRH agonists work but have significant effects. Surgery, CBT and supplements rest on small or PMS-only evidence, and nothing has been tested specifically in women with ADHD.

    24 min read · for women, clinicians

  • Starting and adjusting ADHD medication as a woman

    Stimulants and atomoxetine are the medicines with consistent adult evidence, but Cochrane rates much of it low certainty and the trials skew male. Appetite, sleep and heart-rate effects are common and need monitoring. About half of adults stop within a year, and women are no more likely to stop. Evidence on sex differences and on premenstrual dosing is thin.

    17 min read · for women, clinicians

  • Living with PMDD: relationships and getting taken seriously

    Self-selected surveys and interviews consistently describe years of dismissal and misdiagnosis, a 'dual identity' and strain on relationships. Daily ratings, the right clinician and partner-inclusive CBT are the practical levers. The evidence is consistent but mostly qualitative.

    12 min read · for women, clinicians

  • Home, money and daily life with ADHD

    Lifetime financial costs of ADHD and the link between inattention and hoarding are documented. Most popular daily-life strategies and labels, such as body doubling, 'revenge bedtime procrastination', doom piles and hygiene struggles, have essentially no research, so this guide should say clearly what is lived experience rather than evidence.

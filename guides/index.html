For women

  • Getting assessed as an adult woman

    How adult ADHD assessment works for women, why women are recognised late, and what screening tools can and cannot tell you.

  • Preparing your assessment pack

    A checklist for assembling childhood evidence, self-report scores, DRSP charts and an impact summary before an ADHD assessment.

  • Masking & high achievement: the "gifted kid" paradox

    Why high IQ does not protect against ADHD, why tests can look normal, and what the (still thin) evidence on masking says.

  • Tracking your cycle with the DRSP

    How to track symptoms daily with the DRSP for at least two cycles, the only accepted route to a PMDD diagnosis.

  • ADHD + PMDD: what the evidence says

    The ADHD-PMDD association is consistent in direction but its size (roughly 2-6x on retrospective screens) has never been confirmed prospectively.

  • Talking to your GP about ferritin

    Iron deficiency without anaemia is common in menstruating women, and iron reduced fatigue in RCTs of women with ferritin of 50 or below.

  • Night owl: delayed sleep timing in ADHD

    Many adults with ADHD have a delayed body clock; melatonin and light shift timing but are not ADHD treatments.

  • Emotion dysregulation and "RSD": what's real

    Emotion dysregulation is well supported in adult ADHD, rejection sensitivity findings are mixed, and RSD is not a validated construct.

  • Gut, pelvis & pain: IBS, endometriosis and ADHD

    ADHD is linked to functional gut problems such as IBS and constipation; endometriosis is often misdiagnosed as IBS; microbiome claims are preliminary.

  • Burnout recovery: what we know and don't

    What burnout means (occupational, autistic, "ADHD burnout"), its heavy overlap with depression, and contributors worth checking.

  • Immune, allergy & hypermobility: linked vs hype

    Atopy and autoimmunity are modestly but robustly linked to ADHD; hypermobility evidence is clinic-based; MCAS and neuroinflammation claims are unsupported.

  • Autism traits, sensory sensitivity & "AuDHD"

    ADHD and autism often co-occur; sensory sensitivity and camouflaging are not unique to autism; online screeners cannot confirm a diagnosis.

  • Contraception, postpartum & perimenopause with ADHD

    Registry evidence on hormonal contraception, postpartum and perimenopause in women with ADHD, and what remains untested.

  • Nail biting, skin picking & other BFRBs

    Body-focused repetitive behaviours as arousal regulation, the strong evidence for habit reversal, and why NAC evidence for nail biting is weak.

  • Body signals: interoception, eating & daily function

    Interoception in ADHD is barely studied and findings conflict, while links between ADHD and eating disorders are well established.

  • ADHD medication in pregnancy and breastfeeding: deciding together

    What large registry studies and meta-analyses show about continuing or stopping ADHD medication in pregnancy, framed as a shared decision, with an honest note on how little breastfeeding data exist.

  • After the birth: perinatal mood when you have ADHD, autism or a PMDD history

    Women with ADHD, autistic women and women with premenstrual disorders all show higher rates of perinatal depression; this guide sets out the evidence and what to put in a perinatal care plan.

  • Diagnosed after my child: ADHD and autism in mothers

    Why mothers are often recognised after their children, what the timing data show, and how a mother's own ADHD affects parenting load and the benefit her child gets from parent training.

  • Midlife: perimenopause, HRT and the ADHD or autistic brain

    What is known about ADHD and autism through perimenopause, the risk window for depression (especially with a premenstrual-mood history), and what HRT trials do and do not show for mood and cognition. Extends G23.

  • Teenage girls with ADHD: late recognition, self-harm and sexual health

    For parents of teenage girls and for clinicians: girls are diagnosed about four years later, adolescence is the peak window for self-harm, and teenage parenthood is more likely, so sexual-health and safety planning belong in ADHD care.

  • Who gets diagnosed? Ethnicity, culture and ADHD/autism in women (incl. India and the South Asian diaspora)

    Diagnosis rates differ by ethnicity, language and sex; Asian-American, Black and Hispanic people are diagnosed less often in the US, and Indian data on adult women are very limited. Graded honestly by setting.

  • Gender-diverse and neurodivergent: what the evidence says

    Autism (and, much less studied, ADHD) is more common in transgender and gender-diverse people; this guide summarises the size of the overlap, expert assessment guidance and the limited evidence on hormones and mood.

  • ADHD after 60: late diagnosis, memory worries and dementia

    ADHD persists into old age and is rarely diagnosed; its symptoms can mimic early cognitive decline, and registry studies link it to later dementia. No study found analyses older women separately.

  • Safety: violence and victimisation in women with ADHD or autism

    Women with ADHD and autistic women report much higher rates of partner and sexual violence; this guide gives the evidence with its limits (small or self-selected samples) and places responsibility on perpetrators, not victims.

  • ADHD, autism and POTS

    What POTS is, why it mostly affects young women, how its brain fog differs from ADHD, what is and isn't known about links with autism, hypermobility, mast cells, the menstrual cycle and iron, and which treatments have evidence.

  • PMDD treatments ranked by evidence

    SSRIs have the strongest evidence and work in the luteal phase alone. A drospirenone 24/4 pill is a modest second-line option. GnRH agonists work but are limited by menopausal effects. Surgery, CBT and supplements rest on small or PMS-only evidence, and nothing has been tested specifically in women with ADHD.

  • Starting and adjusting ADHD medication as a woman

    Stimulants and atomoxetine are the medicines with consistent adult evidence, but Cochrane rates much of it low certainty and the trials skew male. Appetite, sleep and heart-rate effects are common and need monitoring. About half of adults stop within a year, and women are no more likely to stop. Evidence on sex differences and on premenstrual dosing is thin.

  • Living with PMDD: relationships and getting taken seriously

    Self-selected surveys and interviews consistently describe years of dismissal and misdiagnosis, a 'dual identity' and strain on relationships. Daily ratings, the right clinician and partner-inclusive CBT are the practical levers. The evidence is consistent but mostly qualitative.

  • Home, money and daily life with ADHD

    Lifetime financial costs of ADHD and the link between inattention and hoarding are documented. Most popular daily-life strategies and labels, such as body doubling, 'revenge bedtime procrastination', doom piles and hygiene struggles, have essentially no research, so this guide should say clearly what is lived experience rather than evidence.

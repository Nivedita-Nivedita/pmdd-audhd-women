For clinicians: the connected-body checklist

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short

  • Women with ADHD can present with physical problems that are common, treatable and often missed. Ask about symptoms and investigate when the answers point that way; this is not a routine test battery for everyone with ADHD. Examples: iron deficiency without anaemia Vaucher 2012 [S], delayed sleep phase and RLS Coogan 2017 [S], functional GI symptoms Blake 2026 [S], and pelvic pain that may be endometriosis Ballard 2008 [S].

  • Eating disorders (pooled odds ratio 3.82) Nazar 2016 [E] and mild injuries, highest in women with ADHD Libutzki 2022 [S], are worth asking about directly.

  • In one UK primary-care cohort, estimated life expectancy was 8.64 years shorter for women with diagnosed ADHD, which the authors believe is likely due to modifiable risk factors and unmet support needs O'Nions 2025 [S]. This is a population average, not an individual forecast.

Evidence grade: SUPPORTED (S) overall. Individual items graded inline; ADHD × endometriosis and hypermobility are PRELIMINARY (P). Use: the one-page checklist is under "What to do in practice". Not medical advice.

If you are thinking about suicide or are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a local crisis line now. Don't wait for it to pass. See crisis support.

1. Ferritin and bleeding history in fatigued women

  • Non-anaemic women with unexplained fatigue (n = 144): iron reduced fatigue 29% vs 13% on placebo over 4 weeks; benefit confined to ferritin ≤ 50 µg/L (subgroup finding) Verdon 2003 [S].
  • Menstruating women, ferritin < 50, Hb > 12 (n = 198): fatigue −47.7% vs −28.8% at 12 weeks (difference −18.9%); no clear difference was found in mood or quality of life in this single trial Vaucher 2012 [S].
  • 18 RCTs (1,170 participants), iron-deficient non-anaemic adults: self-reported fatigue SMD −0.38; no gain in objective physical capacity Houston 2018 [S].
  • Diagnosis requires ferritin and/or transferrin saturation; oral iron first line, IV for blood loss or malabsorption Auerbach 2025 [E].
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding is a major, under-recognised cause of iron deficiency Munro 2023 [S].
  • ADHD link: ferritin lower in children with ADHD (SMD −0.40; no clear difference in serum iron) Wang 2017 [S]. Adult data are sparse.

Practical tip. Frame iron deficiency for patients as a treatable comorbidity, not a cause of ADHD.

2. Sleep timing and RLS

  • Later chronotype and delayed DLMO/sleep onset are consistent in ADHD (62 studies, 4,462 with ADHD) Coogan 2017 [S].
  • Adult ADHD: worse subjective sleep (SMD 0.56–1.55) and longer actigraphic latency (SMD 0.80), but no polysomnographic differences, so a normal PSG and a genuine sleep complaint can coexist Díaz-Román 2018 [S].
  • Insomnia in 43–80% of adults with ADHD; treatment-emergent insomnia in 10–45% on various stimulants Wynchank 2017 [S].
  • RLS in 33.0% of 200 adults with ADHD; ferritin < 50 ng/mL in 35.5%, more frequent with RLS Lopez 2019 [S]. RLS symptoms in 20–33.0% of adults with ADHD across 29 studies Migueis 2023 [S].
  • IRLSSG: IV ferric carboxymaltose effective for moderate–severe RLS with ferritin < 300 µg/L; oral iron possibly effective with ferritin ≤ 75 µg/L Allen 2018 [E].
  • Low-dose melatonin advanced DLMO by 1 h 28 min in 51 adults with ADHD + DSPS; symptom effect (−14%) disappeared 2 weeks after stopping van Andel 2021 [P].

3. Functional GI symptoms; pelvic pain should prompt thinking about endometriosis

  • ADHD and GI symptoms (23 studies, > 1.9 million): IBS odds ratio 1.58, constipation odds ratio 1.97; organic GI disease studies were excluded, not tested Blake 2026 [S].
  • Young adults (33,380 ADHD vs 355,652): IBS odds ratio 1.67, constipation odds ratio 1.64; no clear association with IBD or coeliac disease Kedem 2020 [S]. But in Norwegian registers, women (not men) with ADHD had higher odds of Crohn's (adjusted odds ratio 1.44) and UC (1.28) Hegvik 2018 [P].
  • Endometriosis cases vs controls (UK GPRD, 5,540 cases): odds ratios of abdominopelvic pain 5.2, dysmenorrhoea 8.1, menorrhagia 4.0, IBS 1.6 Ballard 2008 [S]. Women with endometriosis were 3.5 times as likely to have an IBS diagnosis and 2.5 times as likely to get a new one after endometriosis was confirmed (the abstract does not name the ratio type) Seaman 2008 [S].
  • Mean diagnostic delay 6.7 years, mainly in primary care Nnoaham 2011 [S]; median 10.4 years, 74% with at least one false diagnosis Hudelist 2012 [S].
  • ESHRE challenges laparoscopy/histology as the gold-standard diagnostic test Becker 2022 [E].
  • ADHD × endometriosis: bidirectional association in Swedish registers (sibling-adjusted hazard ratio up to 1.98 for ADHD) Gao 2020 [P]. Single study.

4. Atopy and autoimmunity history

  • Childhood eczema (odds ratio 1.31, class I) and asthma (odds ratio 1.51, class II) Kim 2020 [E]. Asthma odds ratio 1.45 in Swedish registers after adjustment for identified confounders Cortese 2018 [E].
  • Danish cohort: personal autoimmune disease incidence rate ratio 1.24; maternal 1.12; no clear association for paternal Nielsen 2017 [S]. Psoriasis adjusted odds ratio 1.57 in females Hegvik 2018 [S].
  • First-generation H1 antihistamines impair attention and vigilance with next-day carryover; second-generation largely do not Kay 2000 [E]. Practical tip: bear this in mind when choosing an antihistamine for a patient with ADHD.
  • Generalised joint hypermobility adjusted odds ratio 4.7 in adult ADHD (431 vs 417) Glans 2021 [P]; no clear association in a school sample of 207 11-year-olds Glans 2025 [P].

5. Eating disorders (pooled odds ratio 3.82)

  • 17 studies: ADHD associated with any eating disorder (pooled odds ratio 3.82; AN 4.28, BN 5.71, BED 4.13); stronger with interview-diagnosed ADHD (5.89) than self-report (2.23). People with eating disorders had higher odds of ADHD (odds ratio 2.57; 5.77 in binge-eating cohorts) Nazar 2016 [E].
  • US NCS-R: in women, bulimia nervosa and lifetime anorexia nervosa were associated with ADHD, and ADHD invariably began before the eating disorder Brewerton 2016 [S].
  • Adult obesity odds ratio 1.55 (28.2% vs 16.4%); no clear excess risk was found in individuals medicated for ADHD Cortese 2016 [E].

6. Accidents and injuries

  • German claims data (17,041 adults with ADHD, 1:4 matched): raised risk of mild and severe accidents and injuries across adulthood; women with ADHD had the highest risk of mild incidents at all ages; ADHD medication was associated with lower risk Libutzki 2022 [S].
  • Within-individual analysis (2,319,450 US patients): medicated months had lower risk of crash-related emergency visits, odds ratio 0.58 in women Chang 2017 [E].
  • Swedish registers: serious transport accidents hazard ratio 1.45 in women with ADHD; medication was not significantly associated with risk in women Chang 2014 [S]. The women-specific medication effect is therefore not settled.

7. Life expectancy context: unmet needs

These figures are averages for diagnosed groups in particular countries and periods. They describe risk that is largely modifiable, not a fixed outcome for any patient.

  • UK primary care (30,039 adults with diagnosed ADHD vs 300,390 matched): estimated life expectancy 6.78 years shorter for men and 8.64 years for women; the authors believe this is likely due to modifiable risk factors and unmet support needs O'Nions 2025 [S].
  • Danish cohort: fully adjusted mortality rate ratio 2.07, mainly unnatural causes, especially accidents; after excluding ODD, conduct disorder and substance use disorder, the mortality rate ratio was 2.85 in girls and women vs 1.27 in boys and men Dalsgaard 2015 [S].
  • Starting ADHD medication within 3 months of diagnosis was associated with lower 2-year all-cause mortality (hazard ratio 0.79), driven by unnatural causes Li 2024 [S]. Observational data, so this does not show medication itself caused the difference; medication decisions weigh individual benefits and harms with the prescriber.

What to do in practice

One-page checklist

Use it symptom-led: ask the questions, and test or refer only when the answers, examination or history point that way.

Domain Ask Consider Grade
Iron Fatigue? Concentration? Bleeding days, flooding, clots, night-time leaks? If symptoms or bleeding suggest it: ferritin and/or transferrin saturation, not FBC alone Auerbach 2025. Assess heavy menstrual bleeding Munro 2023. S
Sleep timing Usual sleep onset on work vs free days? Trouble falling asleep? Winter worsening? Delayed phase is common Coogan 2017. Sleep diary/MCTQ; review stimulant timing; sleep referral if delayed phase suspected. S (timing) / P (chronotherapy)
RLS Urge to move legs in the evening? If yes: ferritin; IRLSSG thresholds apply to RLS treatment, not general targets Allen 2018. S
Gut IBS-type symptoms, constipation? Cycle-linked? IBS and constipation more common in ADHD Blake 2026; don't assume functional in women (female-only IBD signal Hegvik 2018). S / P
Pelvis Dysmenorrhoea, chronic pelvic pain, menorrhagia, prior IBS label? Think endometriosis Ballard 2008; imaging and gynaecology referral per local pathway. S
Atopy / autoimmune Eczema, asthma, psoriasis, IBD, thyroid; family history? Modest associations Kim 2020; prefer second-generation antihistamines Kay 2000. E/S
Hypermobility Joint pain, sprains, dizziness on standing? Clinic-sample association Glans 2021; Beighton score. P
Eating Binge eating, restriction, purging, weight change? Screen directly; pooled odds ratio 3.82 for any eating disorder Nazar 2016. E
Injuries Recent injuries, near-misses, driving incidents? Women with ADHD: highest mild-injury risk Libutzki 2022. Discuss safety and treatment. S
Overall Unmet support needs? Population-level reduced life expectancy is attributed by the authors to modifiable risks and unmet needs O'Nions 2025. S

Practical tip. Ask about bleeding and sleep with specific questions, not "any problems with periods or sleep?". Patients with long-standing symptoms often normalise them.

Practical tip. Record the actual ferritin value and the threshold used. Patients may compare with published trial cut-offs.

Practical tip. Iron, melatonin and ADHD medication are prescriber decisions: treat a confirmed deficiency, recheck rather than continuing iron indefinitely, and review side effects and interactions.

Talk to a clinician about…

(For the reviewing clinician: points of referral or shared care.)

  • Haematology or gynaecology input when iron deficiency recurs or bleeding is heavy.
  • Sleep medicine for suspected delayed sleep–wake phase disorder or refractory RLS.
  • Gynaecology for persistent pelvic pain, regardless of a prior IBS diagnosis.
  • Gastroenterology when red-flag or organic features are present.
  • Eating-disorder services when screening is positive.
  • The prescriber of ADHD medication, for sleep effects and injury risk.

What we don't know yet

  • Adult women with ADHD are rarely studied separately for iron, sleep timing, GI symptoms or atopy; most iron and immune data are paediatric.
  • Whether treating these comorbidities improves ADHD outcomes. The iron–ADHD treatment evidence is one RCT of 23 children; chronotherapy has one adult RCT of about 50 people.
  • ADHD × endometriosis rests on a single register study.
  • Women-specific medication effects on accidents are inconsistent between studies.
  • Eating disorders: we did not find an eligible study in our search (last updated October 2026) on how ADHD treatment interacts with eating-disorder treatment in women.

Common claims: what the research supports

  • In menstruating women with low ferritin but normal haemoglobin, iron reduced self-reported fatigue in randomised trials [E]. More in Claim check
  • Many people with ADHD have a later body clock, with delayed melatonin onset, eveningness and later, slower sleep onset [S]. More in Claim check
  • IBS and constipation are more common in people with ADHD [S]. Functional GI; Blake 2026 excluded organic disease rather than testing it. More in Claim check
  • Women with endometriosis are often given an IBS diagnosis first, and diagnosis commonly takes years [S]. More in Claim check
  • Asthma and eczema are modestly more common in children with ADHD, but food allergy has not shown a link [E]. More in Claim check
  • In UK and Danish population data, adults with diagnosed ADHD died earlier on average, with a larger estimated gap for women [S]. An average, not a personal forecast. More in Claim check

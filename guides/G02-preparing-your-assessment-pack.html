Preparing your assessment pack

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short

  • Everyday difficulties count for more than test scores, so your pack should show how ADHD-type problems affect your real life, and what coping costs you.

  • Childhood evidence matters: school reports, and a parent or sibling who can describe you as a child.

  • If your symptoms change with your cycle, start daily tracking now. Telling PMDD apart from premenstrual worsening needs at least two cycles of daily ratings, but you don't need to delay your assessment until they're finished.

Evidence grade: SUPPORTED (S). The questionnaires below describe your profile. None of them diagnoses anything.

Talk to a clinician about… see the section near the end.

Not medical advice.

If you are thinking about suicide or are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a local crisis line now. Don't wait for your assessment or for it to pass: crisis support.

Tags: [E] established · [S] supported · [P] preliminary · [U] popular but unsupported. Practical tip and Process note paragraphs are general guidance and carry no grade.

An assessment appointment is short compared with a lifetime. A well-organised pack helps the assessor see the whole picture. This guide goes with G01, Getting assessed as an adult woman.

Process note. The pack is optional, and it doesn't need to be complete. Bring what is easy to gather. You don't need to fill in every questionnaire below: clinicians use their own validated tools, and one or two pages about your everyday life are often more useful than a stack of scores.

1. What clinicians weigh: real-world impairment over test scores

Lab tests of executive function (planning, focus, self-control) and ratings of everyday behaviour agree poorly. Across 20 studies the median correlation was r = .19, and the authors concluded they measure different things Toplak 2012 [E]. In people followed from childhood hyperactivity to a mean age of 27, self-reported everyday problems predicted impairment at home and work better than tests did Barkley 2011 [S]. In a study of adults at work, ADHD-related problems mostly took the form of not meeting their own standards and potential Fuermaier 2021 [S]. Test scores did not predict how well they functioned at work Fuermaier 2021 [S].

So your pack should focus on what happens in your life, not on proving you can't concentrate in a quiet room.

2. Childhood evidence

Process note. Under DSM-5, some symptoms must have been present before age 12. Assessors therefore want evidence from childhood. Expert guidance on women recommends a lifespan view, looking for the subtler, internalised presentation common in girls Young 2020 [E].

What counts: - School reports. Girls' reports may not mention disruptive behaviour. In children, diagnosis followed disruptive behaviour more strongly in girls than in boys, so quieter girls may be missed Mowlem 2018 [S]. Look instead for comments like "daydreams", "capable but doesn't apply herself", "chatty", "disorganised" or "rushed work". - An informant. This is a parent, sibling or other relative who can describe you before age 12. If your assessor wants their view, they may send an informant form or invite them to join part of the interview. Ask which form they'd like used rather than adapting a self-report questionnaire. - Your own memories. Note specific examples, not general feelings.

Practical tip. If no one from your childhood is available, say so; it isn't a dead end. Bring whatever indirect evidence you have: reports, old diaries, or messages describing you. For adults first assessed for autism, the lack of a childhood informant is a recognised difficulty Lai 2015 [S], so you won't be the first person without one.

3. Self-report tools to complete beforehand

These describe your profile. None diagnoses ADHD. A high score means "worth discussing". A low score doesn't rule ADHD out.

Process note. Access details come from our questionnaire guide. Where licence terms couldn't be confirmed, we say so. Use the official or free versions from the sources named below, and don't copy or share copyrighted questionnaires. Ask your assessor first: many will send their own forms, so you may not need any of these. If you do fill some in, one or two is plenty; there's no need to collect more.

Tool What it covers Access Evidence note
ASRS v1.1 Current adult ADHD symptoms; 18 items, 6 of which form a screener 6-item screener free (NYU licence page) Screening only. Its wording follows the older DSM-IV. In the original validation in a US general-population survey sample (154 respondents), the six-question screener had 68.7% sensitivity and 99.5% specificity, so it misses some people with ADHD: a negative screen does not rule ADHD out Kessler 2005 [E].
WURS-25 Childhood ADHD symptoms, recalled ("As a child I was…") Free PDF hosted by CADDRA; "public domain" status not confirmed Relies on memory. In the original study, a cutoff of 46 identified 86% of 81 adult ADHD outpatients, 99% of 100 adults without ADHD and 81% of 70 adults with depression Ward 1993 [S]. Claims that it was developed in men couldn't be verified from the original abstract. It may favour disruptive symptoms over inattentive ones (we haven't checked the item wording). It was designed for you to complete about yourself; in the original study, scores correlated moderately with parents' ratings Ward 1993 [S].
MEWS Excessive, hard-to-control mind wandering Items printed in the open-access paper; formal licence not confirmed In two adult ADHD studies it had about .9 sensitivity and specificity, and mind wandering added to impairment beyond core symptoms Mowlem 2016 [S].
ACI Cognitive disengagement syndrome (CDS): daydreaming, mental fog, slowness Items in the published paper; licence not confirmed An international work group concluded CDS is a distinct syndrome whose nature is still unclear Becker 2023 [S]. CDS is not a DSM diagnosis.
DERS-16 Difficulties regulating emotions Free; items in the paper Not ADHD-specific. Emotion dysregulation is common in adult ADHD: across 13 studies, adults with ADHD scored much higher than controls (Hedges' g = 1.17) Beheshti 2020 [S].
MCTQ Sleep timing on work days and free days Free (LMU Munich); licence terms not confirmed Gives clock times. A review of 62 studies found consistent evidence of later chronotype and delayed sleep timing in ADHD Coogan 2017 [S].

Practical tip. - Follow each questionnaire's instructions and answer accurately. Separately, describe your workarounds and what they cost you (see the impact summary below). - Print your scores and bring them. - Don't use these to self-diagnose. - If you feel low or anxious while filling them in, pause.

4. Bring two or more cycles of daily ratings (PMDD vs PME)

If your mood, focus or overwhelm gets worse before your period, daily tracking is the strongest thing you can add.

  • Why daily, and why two cycles. PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder) is diagnosed from daily ratings over at least two cycles. A standard scoring system called the C-PASS matched expert diagnosis with an estimated 98% correct classification in 200 women Eisenlohr-Moul 2017 [E]. In the same study, women's recollections of premenstrual worsening poorly predicted the diagnosis that daily ratings gave Eisenlohr-Moul 2017 [E].
  • One-off questionnaires overestimate PMDD. Pooled prevalence was 7.7% with provisional (one-off) diagnosis, against 3.2% with confirmed diagnosis Reilly 2024 [S].
  • PMDD isn't the only possibility. Worsening of an existing condition before a period is called premenstrual exacerbation (PME). Around 60% of women with mood disorders report it Kuehner 2021 [S]. In a small daily study of 32 young women, day-to-day changes in hormone levels predicted next-day ADHD symptoms Roberts 2018 [P].

Process note. - The DRSP (Daily Record of Severity of Problems) is the standard daily form. The International Association for Premenstrual Disorders (IAPMD) offers a free DRSP-based tracker. - The C-PASS scoring worksheet is free from the UNC Center for Women's Mood Disorders. - Telling PMDD apart from PME still needs a clinician. We couldn't confirm that the C-PASS alone identifies PME.

Practical tip. - Add a daily line for ADHD symptoms (focus, forgetting, overwhelm). - Mark your period days. - Note any stimulant or hormonal contraception, and changes to either.

See G04 for a full tracker how-to.

5. Write a one-page "impact summary"

This is the most useful page in your pack. It shows the cost of compensating, which test scores and symptom checklists miss. Adults with ADHD describe many self-made coping strategies used before diagnosis, and this coping may help explain late diagnosis Canela 2017 [P].

Practical tip: a template. - Work or study: what you achieve, and what it takes. For example: "I meet deadlines by working until 2 am the night before. Weekly, about __ extra hours." - Home and admin: bills, forms, appointments, food and washing. What slips? - Relationships: missed messages, forgotten plans, conflict, withdrawing. - Health and energy: sleep, exhaustion, weekends spent recovering, skipped meals. - Emotions: overwhelm, irritability, shutdown, and whether these follow a cycle. - Workarounds: your systems, and what happens when they break. - Childhood: two or three concrete examples. - What you've already tried: for example, treatment for anxiety or depression, and how far it helped.

Keep it to one page, with bullet points. Lead with your three biggest impacts.

6. Questions to ask the assessor

Practical tip. You might ask: 1. Will you use a structured interview, such as the DIVA-5? 2. Will you speak to an informant, and can it be someone other than a parent? 3. How will you weigh my everyday functioning against test scores? 4. Which other conditions are you considering: anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder, BPD, autism, sleep problems, PME? 5. Will I get a written report, and when? 6. If the answer isn't ADHD, what do you think explains my difficulties, and where can I get support?

What you can do

Practical tips: your assessment-pack checklist

Everything here is optional. Tick off what's easy to gather and skip the rest; a short pack is fine.

  • [ ] One-page impact summary
  • [ ] School reports, or a note on why they aren't available
  • [ ] If available: name and contact of an informant who agrees to take part
  • [ ] Optional: your ASRS v1.1 and WURS-25, scored, if your assessor hasn't sent their own forms
  • [ ] Optional profile tools only if they're easy and relevant: MEWS, ACI, DERS-16, MCTQ
  • [ ] Daily DRSP ratings, if symptoms vary with your cycle (start now; bring what you have, ideally two or more cycles, without delaying the appointment)
  • [ ] List of current medicines, including hormonal contraception
  • [ ] Past diagnoses and treatments, and how well they worked
  • [ ] Your questions for the assessor
  • [ ] A copy of everything for yourself

Talk to a clinician about…

  • Which questionnaires they want you to complete, so you don't duplicate them.
  • Whether your daily ratings suggest PMDD, PME of ADHD or mood symptoms, or neither.
  • Who can serve as an informant if parents aren't available.
  • Low mood, anxiety or thoughts of self-harm, now, without waiting for the assessment.

What we don't know yet

  • How well these questionnaires work in women who mask, or in high-IQ adults. We did not find an eligible study validating screener cut-offs in gifted adults, or testing them in women who compensate, in our search (last updated October 2026).
  • How the ASRS and WURS perform in women today. The original validation studies are cited above, but they were not designed to report results separately for women who mask. Whether the WURS was developed mainly in men is unverified.
  • Licence terms for the MEWS, ACI and MCTQ couldn't be confirmed.
  • Whether an impact summary changes assessment outcomes. It is practical advice; we did not find a study testing it in our search (last updated October 2026).
  • Whether the C-PASS alone can identify PME. This is unconfirmed, so a clinician's judgement is still needed.

Popular claims checked

  • online-quiz-can-diagnose-adhd-or-autism: "An online quiz can tell you whether you have it." False (established).
  • hormone-test-diagnoses-pmdd: "A hormone blood test or a one-off quiz can tell you if you have PMDD." False (established). Daily tracking over at least two cycles is needed.
  • premenstrual-worsening-means-pmdd: "If your ADHD gets worse before your period, that's PMDD." Overstated (established). It is often PME, which is different.
  • high-iq-masks-adhd-on-tests: "A high IQ masks ADHD on testing." Partly true (preliminary). Real-life impairment matters more than test results.

Immune, allergy & hypermobility: linked vs hype

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short

  • Eczema and asthma are modestly more common with ADHD, mostly shown in children Kim 2020 [E]. Autoimmune disease shows smaller links, and some appear in women only Hegvik 2018 [P]. These are links, not causes.

  • Very flexible joints (hypermobility) are much more common in adults with ADHD seen in clinics Glans 2021 [P], but one school study of 11-year-olds found no link Glans 2025 [P].

  • "MCAS drives ADHD" and "ADHD is a neuroinflammatory disorder" go beyond the evidence Valent 2012 [U]; Misiak 2022 [U].

Evidence grade: ESTABLISHED/SUPPORTED (E/S) for modest atopy and autoimmunity associations. PRELIMINARY (P) for hypermobility. POPULAR BUT UNSUPPORTED (U) for MCAS and "neuroinflammatory disorder" claims. Talk to a clinician about: which antihistamine you take, joint pain or dizziness on standing, and any autoimmune conditions in you or your family. Not medical advice.

A few terms first:

  • Atopy: a tendency to allergic conditions such as eczema, asthma and hay fever.
  • Autoimmune disease: when the immune system attacks the body's own tissue, for example psoriasis, type 1 diabetes or Crohn's disease.
  • Odds ratio (OR), risk ratio (RR/IRR) and hazard ratio (HR): ways of comparing two groups. A value of 1 means no difference. Above 1 means more common in the first group.

1. Eczema and asthma: convincing but modest associations

  • An umbrella review (a review of 63 meta-analyses) rated the link between childhood eczema and ADHD (OR 1.31) as "convincing" (class I). The link with childhood asthma (OR 1.51) was rated "highly suggestive" Kim 2020 [E].
  • A meta-analysis plus a Swedish study of 1,575,377 people found a pooled adjusted OR of 1.53 for ADHD and asthma. In the Swedish registers the link held after adjusting for all the confounders the authors identified (OR 1.45) Cortese 2018 [E].
  • In children followed over time, allergic conditions came before ADHD: pooled OR 1.34 for asthma, 1.32 for eczema and 1.52 for hay fever (allergic rhinitis) Schans 2017 [S].
  • Children with ADHD had higher odds of asthma (OR 1.80), but food allergy was not linked (OR 1.13, not significant). The authors rated the evidence quality low Miyazaki 2017 [S].

What this means. These odds ratios are modest. Almost all of this evidence is in children. It doesn't show that allergies cause ADHD.

2. Autoimmune disease: personal and maternal links, and a women-only gut signal

  • In 983,680 people in Denmark, having an autoimmune disease went with a higher chance of ADHD (IRR 1.24). Having a mother with one did too (IRR 1.12). Having a father with one did not Nielsen 2017 [S].
  • A cohort of 63,050 plus a meta-analysis of 5 studies linked maternal autoimmune disease with ADHD in children (cohort HR 1.30; meta-analysis HR 1.20). Type 1 diabetes (HR 1.53), hyperthyroidism (1.15) and psoriasis (1.31) in mothers were also linked Nielsen 2021 [S].
  • In Norway, maternal multiple sclerosis (OR 1.8), rheumatoid arthritis (1.7), type 1 diabetes (1.6), asthma (1.5) and underactive thyroid (1.2) were linked to ADHD in children. Maternal high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes were not Instanes 2017 [S].
  • In 2,500,118 people in Norwegian registers Hegvik 2018:
  • ADHD was linked to psoriasis in women (adjusted OR 1.57) and men (1.31) [S].
  • In women only, ADHD was linked to Crohn's disease (1.44) and ulcerative colitis (1.28). Men with ADHD had lower odds of Crohn's [P].
  • Fever or infection during pregnancy was not, overall, linked to ADHD in 89,146 Danish pregnancies (aHR 1.03 and 1.01). There were only isolated signals at specific times, such as fever in weeks 9–12 (aHR 1.33) Werenberg Dreier 2016 [S].
  • A review describes how a mother's immune state in pregnancy might affect brain development. Much of that evidence is early or from animals Han 2021 [P].

What this means. The links are consistent but small. These studies don't establish why they exist.

3. Antihistamines and attention: older vs newer

  • Histamine-producing brain cells are active during waking and help maintain wakefulness and attention Haas 2008 [E].
  • First-generation antihistamines (older, "drowsy" ones that enter the brain) impair divided attention, working memory, vigilance and speed, with effects carrying into the next day. Second-generation ("non-drowsy") antihistamines largely do not Kay 2000 [E].
  • In a crossover trial of 40 people, diphenhydramine 50 mg (a first-generation antihistamine) impaired simulated driving more than alcohol at about 0.1% blood alcohol. Fexofenadine (second-generation) was no different from placebo. People's own sense of drowsiness did not predict how impaired they were Weiler 2000 [S].
  • In 154 children, those with eczema but no ADHD who had used antihistamines had more ADHD symptoms (OR 1.88). Current eczema severity was not linked Schmitt 2018 [P].

Antihistamines have not been studied in adults with ADHD in our library. The general finding still matters: older sedating antihistamines impair attention in people without ADHD, and people may not feel how impaired they are Weiler 2000 [S]. If you already struggle with attention, it is worth knowing.

4. Hypermobility: strong in adult clinics, null in one school population

Hypermobility means joints that move further than usual. It is often measured with the Beighton score, a quick check of how far certain joints bend. Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS) are inherited connective-tissue conditions that include hypermobility.

Adults

  • In 431 adults with ADHD and 417 controls, ADHD was linked to generalised joint hypermobility on examination (adjusted OR 4.7) and to symptomatic hypermobility (adjusted OR 6.9) Glans 2021 [P].
  • In 109 neurodivergent adults, 51% had generalised hypermobility, compared with 20% in the general population (OR 4.51). The odds were greater in women. The number of hypermobile joints helped explain links with dizziness on standing (orthostatic intolerance) and pain Csecs 2022 [P].
  • In Swedish registers, people with EDS had higher risk of ADHD (RR 5.6) and autism (RR 7.4). Their unaffected siblings also had higher ADHD risk (RR 2.1). Results were similar for hypermobility syndrome Cederlöf 2016 [S].

Children in the community

  • In 207 ordinary 11-year-olds in a school sample, high Beighton scores were not linked to neurodevelopmental problems Glans 2025 [P].

Possible bridges. Reviews describe links between hypermobility and anxiety, depression, ADHD, autism and eating disorders Baeza-Velasco 2015 [P]. One proposes coordination problems, fatigue, chronic pain and dizziness as bridges between ADHD and hypermobility Baeza-Velasco 2018 [P]. Separately, 33% of new referrals to a gut clinic had undiagnosed joint hypermobility syndrome, with more indigestion-type symptoms Fikree 2014 [S].

What this means. The link is strong in adults who reach clinics, and in people with diagnosed EDS. It wasn't seen in one community sample of children. We grade this PRELIMINARY until community studies in adults exist.

5. Claim check: MCAS and mast cells

Mast cells are immune cells involved in allergic reactions. Mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS) is a specific diagnosis.

  • Expert consensus criteria for MCAS need three things: typical symptoms, a substantial short-term rise in tryptase or other mast-cell chemicals measured in a lab, and a response to mast-cell-directed treatment Valent 2012 [E].
  • The idea that mast cells contribute to brain inflammation comes mainly from a hypothesis paper about a subgroup of autism. It was based largely on parents' reports of "allergic-like" symptoms Theoharides 2012 [P].
  • Our library contains no peer-reviewed study of mast cells in ADHD that met our quality bar.

So "MCAS drives ADHD" is POPULAR BUT UNSUPPORTED [U]. If you have symptoms that suggest MCAS, the diagnosis needs lab evidence, not just a symptom list Valent 2012 [E].

6. Claim check: "ADHD is neuroinflammatory"

  • A meta-analysis of 10 studies found IL-6 (an inflammation signal) higher in ADHD (g = 0.71) and TNF-alpha lower (g = −0.16). CRP and several other markers did not differ. The overall effects were no longer significant when single studies were removed Misiak 2022 [P].
  • Of 23 blood biomarkers assessed in the umbrella review, only serum vitamin D reached the "highly suggestive" class Kim 2020 [E].
  • One brain-scan (PET) study of 24 people with ADHD and 24 controls found signs of activated immune cells (microglia) in some brain areas. This went with slower processing speed and poorer attention Yokokura 2021 [P].
  • A review sets out five lines of evidence for inflammation as an ADHD risk factor and describes most human strands as initial or preliminary Dunn 2019 [P].

So: there are interesting signals, and the immune-condition links in sections 1 and 2 are real. But "ADHD is a neuroinflammatory disorder" is POPULAR BUT UNSUPPORTED [U].

What you can do

  1. If you need an antihistamine, ask a pharmacist about a non-drowsy (second-generation) option, especially before driving or studying.
  2. If you have joint pain, frequent sprains or dizziness on standing, keep a short note of when it happens.
  3. Write down any autoimmune or allergic conditions in yourself and your close family. It is useful background for any clinician.
  4. Be cautious about tests or supplements sold to "calm inflammation" or "stabilise mast cells" for ADHD.

Talk to a clinician about…

  • Which antihistamine suits you, if you take one regularly.
  • Joint hypermobility, pain or dizziness on standing, and whether assessment or physiotherapy would help.
  • Possible MCAS symptoms, and what testing would actually show.
  • Gut symptoms alongside ADHD (see guide G09), especially given the women-only Crohn's and ulcerative colitis signal.

What we don't know yet

  • Adult women. Almost all the allergy and immune data come from children. There are no cytokine studies in adult women with ADHD.
  • Why the links exist. Shared genes, pregnancy environment and other factors haven't been separated.
  • Antihistamines in ADHD. Not studied directly in adults with ADHD.
  • Hypermobility in the community. Adult evidence comes from clinic or referral samples. The only community study was in children, and it found no link.
  • Mast cells. No ADHD-specific peer-reviewed data in our library.
  • Inflammation. Blood-marker findings are fragile, and the brain-imaging evidence is a single small study.

Popular claims checked

  • adhd-linked-to-allergies-asthma-eczema: "People with ADHD are more likely to have allergies, asthma and eczema." Verdict: mostly true. Modest links, mainly in children. Food allergy shows no link.
  • antihistamines-worsen-adhd-brain-fog: "Antihistamines make ADHD brain fog worse." Verdict: partly true. Older sedating ones impair attention in everyone. Newer ones largely don't.
  • histamine-causes-adhd: "Histamine intolerance or 'high histamine' causes or worsens ADHD." Verdict: unsupported.
  • mcas-drives-adhd: "MCAS drives ADHD and neurodivergence." Verdict: unsupported.
  • adhd-is-neuroinflammation: "ADHD is caused by (neuro)inflammation." Verdict: unsupported.

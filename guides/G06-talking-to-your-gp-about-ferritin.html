Talking to your GP about ferritin

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short

  • Your blood test can come back "normal" for anaemia while your iron stores (ferritin) are low. In two trials, iron reduced tiredness in non-anaemic women with low ferritin Verdon 2003 [S]; Vaucher 2012 [S]. The ferritin cut-offs those trials used (≤ 50 in one, < 50 in the other) were rules for who could take part or which subgroup benefited. They are not a universal treatment target for everyone.

  • Heavy periods are a major, under-recognised cause of low iron Munro 2023 [S]. Your bleeding history matters as much as the blood test.

  • Iron and ADHD: ferritin is a little lower on average in children with ADHD Tseng 2018 [S]. Adult data are thin. The evidence does not establish that low iron causes ADHD, or that iron treats it. Iron is worth checking for its own sake, and it shouldn't be taken long term without a clinician monitoring your levels NHS: Iron deficiency anaemia [Reference].

Evidence grade: SUPPORTED (S) for iron and fatigue in women with low ferritin. PRELIMINARY (P) for iron and ADHD in adults. Talk to a clinician about: a ferritin test, transferrin saturation, your period history, and restless legs. Not medical advice.

If you are thinking about suicide or are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a local crisis line now.

1. "Normal bloods" vs iron deficiency without anaemia

Anaemia means you have too little haemoglobin, the iron-containing protein that carries oxygen in red blood cells. Iron deficiency means your iron stores are low. You can have the second without the first.

  • Iron deficiency can affect how you feel and function even without anaemia Pasricha 2021 [S].
  • In adults, iron deficiency may cause fatigue, exercise intolerance and difficulty concentrating Auerbach 2025 [S].
  • Diagnosing it needs a ferritin test and/or transferrin saturation Auerbach 2025 [E]. Ferritin is a protein that reflects how much iron your body has stored. Transferrin saturation shows how much iron is available in your blood.
  • Ferritin, transferrin saturation and soluble transferrin receptor diagnose iron-deficiency anaemia more accurately than the red-cell measurements on a standard blood count Lopez 2016 [E].
  • Anaemia affects roughly a third of the world's population, half of it due to iron deficiency, and women of childbearing age are at particular risk Lopez 2016 [E].

So "your bloods are normal" may mean "you are not anaemic". It does not always mean your iron stores were checked.

2. What the trials show: fatigue fell in women with low ferritin

Three pieces of evidence point the same way.

  • Verdon 2003 (BMJ). 144 women with unexplained fatigue and no anaemia took iron or a placebo (dummy pill). Over 4 weeks, fatigue fell by 29% on iron vs 13% on placebo. The benefit was confined to women with ferritin ≤ 50 µg/L. This was a subgroup finding, not a pre-set threshold Verdon 2003 [S].
  • Vaucher 2012 (CMAJ). 198 menstruating women had ferritin below 50 and haemoglobin above 12. After 12 weeks of oral iron, fatigue fell by 47.7% vs 28.8% on placebo, a difference of 18.9%. Iron showed no clear change in mood or quality of life Vaucher 2012 [S].
  • Houston 2018 (meta-analysis). Across 18 trials (1,170 people) in iron-deficient adults without anaemia, iron reduced self-reported fatigue (SMD −0.38), but it did not improve objective physical capacity Houston 2018 [S].

An SMD (standardised mean difference) is a way of comparing results across studies that used different scales. In plain terms, iron eased tiredness a little, on average, in iron-deficient adults without anaemia Houston 2018 [S]. Placebo groups also improved in both trials (13% and 28.8%), so some of the change was not due to iron Verdon 2003 [S]; Vaucher 2012 [S].

None of these trials was in women with ADHD.

Trial thresholds are not treatment targets. The "50" in these trials described who was enrolled (Vaucher 2012) or which subgroup benefited in a subgroup analysis, not a pre-set threshold (Verdon 2003) Verdon 2003 [S]; Vaucher 2012 [S]. They don't tell you the ferritin level you personally should aim for, and they don't mean everyone below 50 needs iron. Your clinician will interpret your result alongside your symptoms, blood count, bleeding history and other tests.

3. Heavy periods: an under-recognised cause

Heavy menstrual bleeding is a major, under-recognised cause of iron deficiency and anaemia, with effects on cognition, work and school Munro 2023 [S].

Practical tip. Before your appointment, note how many days you bleed, how often you change pads, tampons or cups on your heaviest day, whether you flood or pass clots, and whether you bleed through at night. Concrete details are easier for a clinician to act on than "heavy".

4. Iron and ADHD: small differences in children; adult data are thin

This is where online claims run ahead of the research.

  • Children, two meta-analyses. Ferritin was lower in children with ADHD (SMD −0.40), but serum iron showed no clear difference (SMD −0.026) Wang 2017 [S]. A second meta-analysis of 17 studies (1,560 children with ADHD, 4,691 controls) found ferritin modestly lower (g = −0.246). Iron deficiency was associated with ADHD (OR 1.636) and with more severe symptoms Tseng 2018 [S].
  • The famous early figure. In one small study, 84% of 53 children with ADHD had ferritin below 30 ng/mL, vs 18% of 27 controls Konofal 2004 [P]. The later meta-analyses found a much smaller average difference.
  • Treatment. In a trial of 23 non-anaemic children with ferritin below 30, 12 weeks of iron reduced scores on one ADHD rating scale (−11.0) vs no change on placebo. Parent and teacher ratings on another scale showed no statistically clear change Konofal 2008 [P].
  • Overall. A systematic review found ferritin results mixed, with only small treatment trials, and noted that iron deficiency may reduce response to stimulant medication Cortese 2012 [P].
  • Adults. In one study of 60 adults with ADHD and 60 controls, ferritin was lower in the ADHD group Gürbüzer 2026 [P]. In 200 adults with ADHD, 35.5% had ferritin below 50 ng/mL Lopez 2019 [P]. That study had no comparison group for this figure.

The honest summary: low iron is a reasonable thing to check, especially if you are tired or have heavy periods. The evidence does not establish that low iron causes ADHD, and iron has not been shown to treat ADHD in adults; the only placebo-controlled trial was in 23 children Konofal 2008 [P].

5. Restless legs, ADHD, and higher ferritin thresholds

Restless legs syndrome (RLS) is an urge to move your legs, usually in the evening or at night, that can make it hard to fall asleep.

  • RLS symptoms were present in 20–33.0% of adults with ADHD (and 11–42.9% of children) across 29 studies Migueis 2023 [S].
  • In 200 adults with ADHD, 33.0% had RLS. Iron deficiency (ferritin < 50 ng/mL) was more common in those with RLS, who also had earlier, more severe ADHD Lopez 2019 [S].
  • An international RLS guideline found intravenous (IV) iron effective for moderate-to-severe RLS in adults with ferritin below 300 µg/L, and oral iron possibly effective with ferritin ≤ 75 µg/L. Evidence in children was insufficient Allen 2018 [E].

These higher cut-offs come from a guideline about treating restless legs, decided by a specialist. They are not general targets for everyone, and they are not a reason to take iron without monitoring.

6. What to ask: ferritin, transferrin saturation, and your bleeding history

  • Iron deficiency is diagnosed with ferritin and/or transferrin saturation Auerbach 2025 [E].
  • Oral iron is first line. IV iron is used for blood loss or malabsorption Auerbach 2025 [E].
  • Newer IV iron preparations allow rapid, safe replacement of iron stores Pasricha 2021 [E].

Practical tip. Ask: "Could you check my ferritin and transferrin saturation, not just my blood count?" Then ask: "What is my actual ferritin number, and what cut-off are you using?" Lab "normal ranges" vary. Knowing the number helps the conversation, but the trial thresholds above are not a target to aim for on your own.

7. Why not to self-supplement indefinitely

  • High doses of oral iron trigger a hormone called hepcidin, which limits how much iron you absorb Pasricha 2021 [S]. More is not automatically better.
  • Side effects. Iron tablets commonly cause constipation or diarrhoea, tummy pain, heartburn, feeling sick and darker poo NHS: Iron deficiency anaemia [Reference]. High doses of iron supplements can cause constipation, nausea, vomiting and stomach pain NHS: Iron [Reference].
  • Overdose. Very high doses of iron can be fatal, particularly in children. An overdose of iron in a young child can be fatal, so keep iron tablets out of children's reach NHS: Iron [Reference]; NHS: Iron deficiency anaemia [Reference].
  • Monitoring. When iron is prescribed for deficiency, the NHS describes repeat blood tests over the following months to check that iron levels return to normal NHS: Iron deficiency anaemia [Reference]. That is why a planned re-test matters.
  • Iron overload. Some people have an inherited condition, haemochromatosis, in which iron slowly builds up. Untreated, this can damage the liver, joints, pancreas and heart, and people being treated for it are usually advised to avoid iron supplements NHS: Haemochromatosis [Reference]. Symptoms such as tiredness and brain fog can overlap with low iron, which is another reason to test rather than guess NHS: Haemochromatosis [Reference].
  • This guide gives no doses. How much, for how long, and when to re-test are decisions for you and your clinician.

Practical tip. If you take iron, agree with a clinician how long to take it and when to re-test. Low iron also has a cause, such as heavy periods, and that cause deserves attention too.

What you can do

  1. Track your periods for one or two cycles using the details in section 3.
  2. Note your energy, concentration and any restless legs in the evening.
  3. Ask your GP for ferritin and transferrin saturation, and ask for the actual numbers.
  4. If iron is started, ask when you will be re-tested and how long to take it. Don't continue iron indefinitely without a re-test.
  5. Take the one-page sheet below.

One-page sheet: take this to your GP

Why I'm here: I've been tired / struggling to concentrate for ___ months. I'd like my iron stores checked.

Tests I'm asking about: ferritin and transferrin saturation, as well as a full blood count.

My periods: I bleed for days. On my heaviest day I change protection every hours. Flooding / clots / bleeding through at night: yes / no.

Other symptoms: restless or uncomfortable legs in the evening: yes / no. Trouble falling asleep: yes / no.

Background: I have (or think I may have) ADHD / autism. I take: _____.

Evidence I've read (for my GP):

  • Iron reduced fatigue in non-anaemic women; benefit confined to ferritin ≤ 50 µg/L (Verdon 2003, BMJ, doi:10.1136/bmj.326.7399.1124).

  • Menstruating women, ferritin < 50, Hb > 12: fatigue fell 47.7% vs 28.8% on placebo (Vaucher 2012, CMAJ, doi:10.1503/cmaj.110950).

  • Heavy menstrual bleeding as an under-recognised cause of iron deficiency (Munro 2023, AJOG, doi:10.1016/j.ajog.2023.01.017).

  • If restless legs: IRLSSG iron guideline (Allen 2018, Sleep Med, doi:10.1016/j.sleep.2017.11.1126).

Questions: What is my ferritin number? What cut-off are you using? If I start iron, when will we re-test? Should my heavy periods be looked into?

Talk to a clinician about…

  • Ferritin and transferrin saturation, not only a blood count.
  • How heavy your periods are, and whether that needs investigating.
  • Restless legs, especially if you also struggle to fall asleep.
  • How long to take iron, the dose, and when to re-test.
  • Whether low iron might affect how well your ADHD medication works. One review raised this, but it is not settled Cortese 2012 [P].

What we don't know yet

  • Long-term self-supplementing in women with ADHD. We did not find a study of this in our search (last updated October 2026). Official safety information on side effects, overdose and iron overload is summarised in section 7.
  • Women with ADHD. None of the fatigue trials studied women with ADHD. We don't know whether iron helps fatigue or concentration differently in them.
  • Adults. The iron–ADHD meta-analyses are in children. Adult data are a handful of small studies.
  • Treatment for ADHD symptoms. The only placebo-controlled iron trial in ADHD had 23 children.
  • Heavy periods and ADHD. We did not find a study linking heavy menstrual bleeding to ADHD symptoms in our search (last updated October 2026).
  • How common low ferritin is in women in general, and in women with ADHD. We removed a prevalence figure because its source could not be checked against an abstract.

Popular claims checked

  • low-ferritin-causes-fatigue-without-anaemia: "Low ferritin can leave you exhausted even if you're not anaemic, and iron helps." Verdict: mostly true. Supported by two trials and a meta-analysis (sections 2 and 6).
  • low-iron-causes-adhd: "Low iron (ferritin) causes ADHD." Verdict: overstated. Ferritin is slightly lower on average in children with ADHD, and serum iron showed no clear difference (section 4). The evidence does not establish that low iron causes ADHD.

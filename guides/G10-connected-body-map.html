Guides › G10

Everyone

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short - ADHD in women often comes with other conditions: allergies, gut problems, eating disorders, a late body clock, autism, and cycle-related mood changes. Some of these links are well established; others rest on a few small studies. - A link means two things turn up together more often than chance. It does not mean one causes the other. - Use the map to spot things worth raising with a clinician, not to diagnose yourself.

Evidence grade: graded per link. Links range from Established (E) to Preliminary (P), and we list some popular ideas that are Unsupported (U). The page's headline grade is Preliminary (P), because several of the links it covers are preliminary.

If you are thinking about suicide or are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a local crisis line now.

How to read this guide

Every research claim has a source and a grade: [E] established, [S] supported, [P] preliminary, [U] popular but unsupported. Practical tip marks general advice that is not itself evidence. Grades describe the claim, not the paper. Where a link's direction is better supported than its size, we grade them separately.

1. Why "connected body" (and why association is not cause)

Many women with ADHD describe a cluster of problems that seem unrelated: eczema as a child, gut trouble, exhausting periods, sleeping late, and being told each one is "just stress". This guide pulls the research on those links into one place.

There are good reasons to look. In UK primary-care records, adults with diagnosed ADHD had an estimated life expectancy 8.64 years shorter for women and 6.78 years shorter for men than matched adults; the authors think this is likely due to modifiable risk factors and unmet support needs O'Nions 2025 [S]. Some of those risks may sit in the conditions on this map.

But nearly all of these links are associations. They come from large registers or surveys that count how often two conditions occur together. They can't tell us whether one causes the other, whether both share a cause (such as genes), or whether people with one condition are simply more likely to be diagnosed with another. When a study goes further (by comparing siblings, for example), we say so.

2. The map

The ten research threads behind this resource are summarised first. Then come the links between them.

The ten threads

  1. Core ADHD in women. ADHD in women is under-recognised rather than a different disorder. Expert consensus names compensatory strategies, gender bias and co-occurring conditions as barriers to recognition Young 2020 [E]. Most apparent "adult-onset" ADHD turned out, on careful assessment, not to be new ADHD Sibley 2018 [S].
  2. Giftedness and masking. High IQ can hide ADHD; it does not protect against it. High-IQ adults with ADHD did worse than high-IQ peers on executive tests, even with scores in the average range Antshel 2010 [S].
  3. Emotion and rejection sensitivity. Emotional dysregulation is much higher in adults with ADHD than in controls (g=1.17) Beheshti 2020 [S]. The popular idea that "rejection sensitive dysphoria" is a core ADHD symptom has no peer-reviewed validation [U] (see "Popular claims checked").
  4. Hormones and PMDD. PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder) is an unusual brain response to normal hormone changes Schmidt 1998 [S]. Diagnosis needs two or more months of daily ratings Eisenlohr-Moul 2017 [S].
  5. Immune system and allergy. Allergy and autoimmune links are real but modest (see section 3). Neuroinflammation as a cause of ADHD is still a hypothesis (see section 5).
  6. Gut and pelvis. IBS, constipation and endometriosis are all linked to ADHD (see section 3).
  7. Iron and energy. Iron reduced fatigue in non-anaemic menstruating women with low ferritin (the body's iron store) Vaucher 2012 [S]. Ferritin is only modestly lower in children with ADHD (g=-0.246) Tseng 2018 [S].
  8. Circadian rhythm and sleep. A late body clock is measurable in many adults with ADHD (see section 3). In children, melatonin shifted sleep earlier but did not improve behaviour or cognition Van der Heijden 2007 [S].
  9. Body and daily function. Eating disorders, sensory sensitivity and repetitive behaviours such as nail biting are part of the picture. Habit reversal therapy had a large effect on repetitive behaviours (d=0.80) Bate 2011 [E].
  10. Autism, sensory processing and burnout. ADHD and autism often co-occur and share genetic risk (see section 3). Studies that screened the general population found a male-to-female odds ratio in autism of 3.25, against 4.56 in studies relying on existing diagnoses; the authors conclude there is a diagnostic bias against girls Loomes 2017 [E]. Burnout's core feature, exhaustion, overlaps heavily with depression Bianchi 2021 [S].

3. Strongest links (Established or Supported)

Atopy (eczema, asthma, hay fever). An umbrella review rated the link between childhood eczema and ADHD as "convincing" (OR 1.31) and childhood asthma as "highly suggestive" (OR 1.51) Kim 2020 [E]. The asthma link held in Swedish registers after adjusting for all identified confounders (OR 1.45) Cortese 2018 [E]. In longitudinal studies, atopic disease came before ADHD Schans 2017 [S]. Most of these data are from children.

Autoimmunity. In a Danish national cohort, having an autoimmune disease was linked to higher ADHD risk (IRR 1.24) Nielsen 2017 [S]. In Norway, ADHD was linked to Crohn's disease (1.44) and ulcerative colitis (1.28) in females only Hegvik 2018 [S]. These are modest, and the female-only signal is unexplained.

Functional gut problems. A meta-analysis of 23 studies found ADHD associated with IBS (OR 1.58) and constipation (OR 1.97); diagnosed organic gut diseases were excluded Blake 2026 [S]. In young adults, ADHD was linked to IBS (OR 1.67) but not to IBD or coeliac disease Kedem 2020 [S].

Endometriosis. In Swedish registers, endometriosis was followed by raised risk of ADHD (sibling-adjusted HR up to 1.98), and earlier ADHD also predicted later endometriosis Gao 2020 [S]. This is one large study and needs replication. Endometriosis often takes years to diagnose; one study found a median of 10.4 years from symptoms to diagnosis Hudelist 2012 [S].

Eating disorders. ADHD was associated with any eating disorder (pooled OR 3.82), including bulimia (5.71) and binge-eating disorder (4.13) Nazar 2016 [E]. In women, ADHD began before the eating disorder Brewerton 2016 [S]. ADHD was also associated with adult obesity (OR 1.55), and people taking ADHD medication were not at higher risk Cortese 2016 [E].

Sleep timing. Adults with ADHD and sleep-onset insomnia (31 of 40) had delayed melatonin onset Van Veen 2010 [S]. A review of 62 studies found consistent evidence for later chronotype in ADHD Coogan 2017 [S].

Autism co-occurrence. ADHD was the most common co-occurring diagnosis in autistic people, at 28% Lai 2019 [E]. In children and adolescents with ADHD, 21% met the threshold for autism Hollingdale 2019 [E]. Co-occurrence in families followed genetic relatedness, consistent with shared genetic liability Ghirardi 2017 [E].

Low ferritin and fatigue. In non-anaemic menstruating women with low ferritin, 12 weeks of oral iron reduced fatigue by 47.7% vs 28.8% on placebo Vaucher 2012 [S]. Heavy menstrual bleeding is a major, under-recognised cause of iron deficiency Munro 2023 [S]. These trials were not in women with ADHD.

Anxiety and depression. Across very large studies, adults with ADHD had much higher odds of anxiety (pooled OR 5.0) and major depression (4.5) Hartman 2023 [E].

Sensory sensitivity. In 116 adults with ADHD, sensory sensitivity tracked ADHD severity but not autistic symptoms, and was reported by 43% of women vs 22% of men Bijlenga 2017 [S].

4. Weaker links (Preliminary)

PMDD: how much more common? The direction is consistent: women with ADHD screen positive for PMDD more often. In one survey, 31.4% of women with self-reported clinical ADHD vs 9.8% without screened positive Broughton 2025 [P]. But the ADHD × PMDD studies so far used one-off questionnaires, and across prevalence studies provisional diagnosis gives higher prevalence estimates than confirmed diagnosis (7.7% vs 3.2%) Reilly 2024 [S]. So the size of the link is preliminary.

Hypermobility. In 431 adults with ADHD vs 417 controls, ADHD was associated with generalised joint hypermobility on examination (adjOR 4.7) Glans 2021 [S]. But in a school sample of 11-year-olds, there was no association Glans 2025 [P]. The evidence comes mainly from clinical samples, so we grade the overall link Preliminary.

Interoception (sensing your body's signals). Two small adult studies disagree: one found a deficit in 14 adults with ADHD Kutscheidt 2019 [P], the other found none in 24 Wiersema 2018 [P]. A review found 3 of 5 comparison studies showed reduced interoception, all small Bruton 2025 [P].

Chronic pain. In 77 women diagnosed with ADHD and/or autism in childhood, 76.6% reported chronic pain at follow-up Asztély 2019 [P]. Genetic analyses found strong overlap between ADHD and multisite chronic pain Ciochetti 2025 [P]. There is no meta-analysis.

ADHD symptoms across the cycle and life stages. In 32 regularly cycling young women, lower estradiol with higher progesterone or testosterone predicted more ADHD symptoms the next day Roberts 2018 [P]. Women with self-reported ADHD had more severe perimenopausal symptoms (54.2% vs 30.1%) Jakobsdóttir Smári 2025 [P].

Camouflaging and exhaustion. Autistic adults, and undiagnosed adults with social difficulties, described social compensation strategies with both benefits and costs, including poor mental health and late diagnosis Livingston 2019 [P]. Whether masking causes burnout has not been shown (see "Popular claims checked").

5. Unsupported ideas (U)

These are popular, but the evidence doesn't back them.

"ADHD is caused by neuroinflammation." [U] A meta-analysis of 10 studies found raised IL-6 and lower TNF-alpha in ADHD, but no differences in CRP and other markers, and the overall effects disappeared when single studies were removed Misiak 2022 [P]. The only direct brain-imaging evidence is one PET study of 24 people with ADHD Yokokura 2021 [P]. Allergy and autoimmune links are real; inflammation as a cause is unproven.

"MCAS drives ADHD." [U] Mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS) has consensus criteria requiring a documented rise in mast-cell mediators such as tryptase and a response to treatment Valent 2012 [E]. The main source for the neurodivergence link is a hypothesis paper about autism based on parent-reported "allergic-like" symptoms Theoharides 2012 [P]. We found no ADHD-specific peer-reviewed data.

"Fix your microbiome to fix ADHD." [U] Microbiome differences across psychiatric disorders were largely shared rather than disorder-specific Nikolova 2021 [P]. In adult ADHD, results varied widely between cohorts Jakobi 2024 [P]. No probiotic trial in our library met the bar for showing benefit.

"Mitochondria are central to neurodivergent burnout." [U] The link has been proposed in a hypothesis paper Mahony 2022 [P], but has not been tested.

6. How to use the map with a clinician

Practical tip. Use the map as a checklist, not a diagnosis. Note which links fit your history, then bring that list to an appointment.

Practical tip. For each item, write down when it started, how often it happens and what it stops you doing. A clinician can act on "I've had constipation most weeks since my teens" far more readily than on "I think I have the ADHD gut thing".

Practical tip. Some items need specific tests or tracking: a ferritin blood test for fatigue, two cycles of daily symptom tracking for premenstrual mood changes (see G04), a sleep diary for a late body clock. Ask which ones apply to you.

Practical tip. Ask "could these be connected?", but accept that the honest answer is often "we don't know yet". The links on this map are mostly associations.

What you can do

  1. Go through the strongest links (section 3) and note which fit you. (Practical tip)
  2. Start a daily symptom diary if your mood or ADHD symptoms seem to follow your cycle. (Practical tip)
  3. Be wary of anyone selling a single cause (inflammation, MCAS, gut bacteria) and a single cure. (Practical tip)
  4. Read the detailed guide for each link that matters to you (G05 PMDD, G06 ferritin, G07 sleep, G09 gut and pelvis, G12 immune, G13 autism, G25 body signals). (Practical tip)

Talk to a clinician about…

  • Fatigue, especially with heavy periods: ask about a ferritin test.
  • Mood or ADHD symptoms that worsen at the same point in each cycle.
  • Gut symptoms, especially with painful periods or painful bowel movements, where endometriosis may be worth considering.
  • Eating patterns that feel out of control, such as binge eating.
  • Sleep that runs persistently late.
  • Possible autism, if social exhaustion and sensory overload are a big part of your life.
  • Joint pain, dizziness on standing, or very flexible joints.

What we don't know yet

  • Cause. For almost every link, we don't know which way it runs or whether a shared factor explains both.
  • Adult women specifically. Much of the immune, iron and gut data comes from children or mixed-sex samples.
  • PMDD size. No ADHD × PMDD study has used daily tracking over two cycles.
  • How the links interact. For example, whether heavy bleeding, low iron and fatigue add up in women with ADHD has not been studied.
  • Whether treating one condition helps another. Few studies test this.
  • Rejection sensitivity and masking. The idea that fear of rejection drives masking has no direct studies.

Popular claims checked

  • adhd-linked-to-allergies-asthma-eczema: "People with ADHD are more likely to have allergies, asthma and eczema." Verdict: mostly true (evidence grade: established).
  • rsd-is-a-core-adhd-symptom: "RSD is a core part of ADHD that almost everyone with ADHD has." Verdict: unsupported (popular but unsupported).
  • adhd-is-neuroinflammation: "ADHD is caused by (neuro)inflammation." Verdict: unsupported (preliminary).
  • mcas-drives-adhd: "MCAS drives ADHD and neurodivergence." Verdict: unsupported (popular but unsupported).
  • gut-microbiome-causes-adhd: "ADHD is caused by gut bacteria, and probiotics can treat it." Verdict: unsupported (preliminary).
  • adhd-and-ibs-go-together: "ADHD and IBS go together." Verdict: mostly true (supported).
  • endometriosis-and-adhd-are-linked: "Endometriosis and ADHD are linked." Verdict: partly true (preliminary).
  • low-iron-causes-adhd: "Low iron (ferritin) causes ADHD." Verdict: overstated (preliminary).
  • low-ferritin-causes-fatigue-without-anaemia: "Low ferritin can leave you exhausted even if you're not anaemic, and iron helps." Verdict: mostly true (supported).
  • adhd-is-a-circadian-disorder: "ADHD is really a body clock disorder." Verdict: overstated (preliminary).
  • adhd-3x-more-likely-pmdd: "Women with ADHD are 3x more likely to have PMDD." Verdict: partly true (preliminary).
  • autism-and-adhd-often-co-occur: "Lots of people with ADHD are also autistic, and vice versa." Verdict: true (established).
  • adhd-means-poor-interoception: "People with ADHD have poor interoception." Verdict: unsupported (preliminary).
  • masking-causes-burnout-and-suicide: "Masking causes autistic burnout and suicide." Verdict: overstated (preliminary).
  • mitochondria-central-to-burnout: "Mitochondria under strain are central to neurodivergent burnout." Verdict: unsupported (popular but unsupported).
  • adhd-shortens-life-expectancy: "ADHD cuts years off your life, especially for women." Verdict: mostly true (supported).

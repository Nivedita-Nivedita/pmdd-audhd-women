Teenage girls with ADHD: late recognition, self-harm and sexual health

Draft. Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Not medical advice.

In short

  • Girls with ADHD are usually diagnosed later than boys. In one Swedish register they were about 4 years older at first diagnosis or medication, and they often had other mental health problems first.

  • Self-harm in girls with ADHD is concentrated in the teenage years. It is worth asking about directly, and kindly.

  • Girls with ADHD were more likely to become teenage parents. Sexual health and contraception advice belong in ADHD care, not outside it.

Evidence grade: SUPPORTED (S) for late diagnosis, self-harm risk and teenage parenthood · PRELIMINARY (P) for earlier puberty in autistic girls · no adequate study of puberty timing in girls with ADHD. Headline grade: SUPPORTED (S).

Talk to a clinician about… a daughter whose struggles have been put down to anxiety or "just being a teenager", any self-harm, and contraception choices.

Not medical advice. Crisis support →

If you are thinking about suicide or are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a local crisis line now.

This guide is mainly for parents and carers of teenage girls, and for the clinicians who see them. "You" means the parent or carer. Where a section is for clinicians, it says so.

Tags: [E] established · [S] supported · [P] preliminary · [U] popular but unsupported. Practical tip paragraphs are general guidance, not graded evidence.

1. Late identification and the comorbidity trail

Many girls with ADHD are noticed late, and the reasons are well described.

  • The size of the delay. In a Stockholm register of 85,330 people with ADHD and 426,626 matched controls, females were older than males at first ADHD diagnosis or medication (23.5 vs 19.6 years), about 4 years later Skoglund 2023 [S].
  • The "comorbidity trail". In the same study, females with ADHD had more other psychiatric diagnoses, more medication and more health-care use than males with ADHD and than female controls, both before and after their ADHD diagnosis Skoglund 2023 [S]. "Comorbidity" means having more than one condition at the same time. In plain terms: many girls get help for something else first.
  • Why girls are missed. A UK expert consensus named symptom differences, gender bias, other conditions and coping strategies that hide symptoms as barriers to recognising ADHD in girls and women Young 2020 [E]. A major review adds that girls show more inattention and more "internalising" problems (such as anxiety and low mood) and are often overlooked because their symptoms are less obvious Hinshaw 2021 [E].
  • Disruptive behaviour drives referral. In 19,804 Swedish twins assessed at age 9, hyperactivity, impulsivity and conduct problems predicted a clinical ADHD diagnosis more strongly in girls than in boys Mowlem 2018 [S]. Girls without those behaviours may be more easily missed Mowlem 2018 [S].

What this means for you: if your daughter has been treated for anxiety, low mood or eating problems and is still struggling with focus, organisation or emotional ups and downs, it is reasonable to ask whether ADHD has been considered.

2. Self-harm: when it peaks and what predicts it

This section talks about self-harm and suicide attempts. It does not describe methods.

The best long-term evidence comes from one US study, the Berkeley Girls ADHD Longitudinal Study. It followed 140 girls with ADHD and 88 girls without ADHD from childhood into adulthood.

  • Risk is raised. At ages 17 to 24, with 95% of the group still taking part, young women who had ADHD as girls had higher rates of suicide attempts and self-injury, with medium to very large effects Hinshaw 2012 [S]. The risk was concentrated in girls with the combined type of ADHD (both inattentive and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms) Hinshaw 2012 [S].
  • The teenage years are the peak. Most self-harm happened in adolescence and had stopped by adulthood Meza 2020 [S].
  • Childhood predictors. Non-suicidal self-injury (hurting yourself without intending to die) was predicted by early behaviour problems, executive-function difficulties and the father's negative parenting Meza 2020 [S]. Suicide attempts were predicted by early behaviour problems, adverse childhood experiences and low self-esteem Meza 2020 [S]. "Executive function" means the mental skills for planning, organising and controlling impulses.
  • The wider picture. Across 57 studies, not limited to girls, ADHD was associated with suicide attempts (OR 2.37) and suicidal ideation (OR 3.53) Septier 2019 [E]. An odds ratio (OR) above 1 means higher odds in the ADHD group. These figures are not specific to teenage girls.

These are associations in groups. They do not mean your daughter will self-harm, and self-harm is not a "symptom" of ADHD. Several of the predictors, such as self-esteem and adversity, are things families and services can work on.

3. Teenage parenthood and unplanned pregnancy

This is about access to information and support, not blame.

  • A large register signal. In a Danish register of 2,698,052 people, including 27,479 with ADHD, females with ADHD were more likely to become parents at 12 to 16 years (incidence rate ratio, IRR, 3.62, 95% CI 2.14-6.13) and at 17 to 19 years (IRR 1.94, 1.62-2.33) than females without ADHD Østergaard 2017 [S]. An IRR of 3.62 means the rate was 3.62 times that of girls without ADHD. The abstract we hold does not give absolute rates, so we can't tell you how many girls this affected. The authors suggest targeted sex education and contraceptive counselling Østergaard 2017 [S].
  • One pathway: school. In the Berkeley cohort, the link between childhood ADHD and unplanned pregnancy by early adulthood ran through low school achievement in early adolescence, which led to riskier sexual behaviour in late adolescence Owens 2019 [P]. This held after adjusting for age, IQ and family income Owens 2019 [P]. It is one cohort, so we grade the pathway preliminary.
  • It persists. In the same cohort at 16-year follow-up, childhood ADHD, whether it later faded or persisted, predicted unplanned pregnancy Owens 2017 [S].
  • Contraception and mood. In Swedish registers of young women aged 15 to 24, women with ADHD had about a 3-fold higher risk of depression whether or not they used hormonal contraception (aHR 3.69) Lundin 2023 [S]. Combined-pill users with ADHD had a higher depression risk than combined-pill users without ADHD (aHR 6.10) Lundin 2023 [S]. In a Danish study of women aged 15 to 34 (not specific to ADHD), adolescents aged 15 to 19 on the combined pill were more likely to start antidepressants than non-users (RR 1.8) Skovlund 2016 [S]. These are associations, not proof that the pill causes depression.

What this means: sexual-health conversations, contraception choice and mood follow-up are part of good ADHD care for teenage girls.

4. Puberty timing and depression

Here the evidence is about girls in general, not girls with ADHD.

  • Early puberty and depression. In 630 girls aged 9 to 16 in the US, early pubertal timing (OR 5.8 after age 12) and higher testosterone (OR 2.0) were the only significant predictors of depression during puberty Copeland 2019 [S]. Oestradiol, age and pubertal stage on their own were not Copeland 2019 [S].
  • Possibly causal, and specific to depression. In 13,398 Norwegian adolescents, a genetic method called Mendelian randomisation suggested a small causal effect: a 29% increase in the odds of a depression diagnosis for each year earlier that periods started Askelund 2024 [S]. There was little consistent evidence of effects on other areas of mental health Askelund 2024 [S].
  • Small effects across the board. A meta-analysis of 101 articles found small but robust links (ds ∼.20) between early puberty and all broad areas of teenage mental health problems, in both sexes Ullsperger 2017 [S]. It reports broad domains, not ADHD.

5. Autistic girls and earlier puberty

In one study of 239 children aged 10 to 13, including 35 autistic girls, autistic girls started puberty earlier than autistic boys and typically developing girls, and started their periods earlier than typically developing girls Corbett 2020 [P]. This is a single small study, so treat it as a signal to watch, not a fact. We found no equivalent study in girls with ADHD.

6. What the evidence does not show: early puberty causing ADHD

You may read that early puberty "causes" or "triggers" ADHD in girls. Our library has no adequate study showing this, and no study of whether girls with ADHD start puberty earlier. The best causal study of age at first period found an effect on depression but little consistent evidence for other areas of mental health Askelund 2024 [S]. ADHD symptoms also begin in childhood, so a teenage "onset" more often means late recognition (see "Popular claims checked").

What you can do

  • Ask about ADHD when other treatment only partly helps. Practical tip: "She's been treated for anxiety, but school, friendships and organisation are still really hard. Could ADHD, or autism, be part of this?"
  • Bring a history. Practical tip: school reports, teacher comments and examples of daily struggles help an assessor see a quieter presentation.
  • Talk about self-harm openly. Practical tip: ask calmly and directly ("Have things ever got so bad you've hurt yourself?"). Listen first, then seek help together.
  • Support self-esteem and school engagement. Low self-esteem predicted suicide attempts, and low school achievement was a pathway to unplanned pregnancy in one cohort Meza 2020 [S] Owens 2019 [P]. Practical tip: school support plans and praise for effort, not just results.
  • Make sexual health routine. Practical tip: ask the GP or ADHD team to include contraception, consent and sexual-health information, as they would for any teenager, with extra attention to options that don't rely on remembering a daily pill.
  • Watch mood after puberty starts early, or after a new contraceptive. Practical tip: a simple mood diary for a few months gives you and the clinician something concrete.

Talk to a clinician about…

  • Whether ADHD or autism has been considered, especially if anxiety or depression treatment has only partly helped.
  • Any self-harm or suicidal thoughts, and a safety plan.
  • Contraception choices, and following up mood after starting hormonal contraception.
  • Low mood that started around early puberty.
  • School support, since school difficulties fed into later risks in one cohort.

What we don't know yet

  • Puberty timing in girls with ADHD. No adequate study tests whether girls with ADHD start puberty earlier, or whether early puberty raises ADHD symptoms. Section 6 of the outline is written as a gap for this reason.
  • Absolute teenage-parenthood rates. The register abstract gives relative rates only.
  • Which interventions reduce self-harm or unplanned pregnancy in girls with ADHD. The predictors are known from one cohort; no intervention trial is in our library.
  • Generalisability. Much of the detailed evidence comes from one US cohort of 140 girls with ADHD. Register findings come from Nordic countries.
  • Autistic girls. Earlier puberty rests on one study with 35 autistic girls.
  • Contraception choice in ADHD. We have association data on mood, but no trial comparing methods in girls with ADHD.

Popular claims checked

  • girls-with-adhd-get-missed: "Girls with ADHD get missed because they're quiet and daydreamy rather than disruptive." Verdict: mostly true. Disruptive behaviour predicted diagnosis more strongly in girls Mowlem 2018 [S], and females were diagnosed about 4 years later Skoglund 2023 [S].
  • adhd-can-start-in-adulthood: "ADHD can start in adulthood." Verdict: overstated. The ledger's better wording is "late recognition", not "late onset". The same applies to claims that ADHD starts at puberty.
  • "Early puberty causes ADHD in girls." (Not yet in the claims ledger.) Verdict: unsupported. No adequate study; the best causal evidence on early menarche shows an effect on depression, with little consistent evidence for other domains Askelund 2024 [S].
  • "Self-harm is just part of ADHD." (Not yet in the claims ledger.) Verdict: misleading. Risk is raised and peaks in adolescence, but it was predicted by modifiable factors such as self-esteem and adversity Meza 2020 [S].

What is PMDD?

Premenstrual dysphoric disorder is a severe, cyclical mood condition. Symptoms such as low mood, irritability, anxiety and feeling out of control build in the days or weeks before a period and ease soon after it starts. It became a full diagnosis in the DSM-5 (Epperson 2012).

It is not a hormone imbalance. Hormone levels are typically normal; the brain reacts differently to normal changes (Schmidt 1998). A 2024 meta-analysis estimated PMDD in about 3.2% of women when the diagnosis was confirmed with daily tracking, about 1.6% in community samples, and about 7.7% when based on one-off questionnaires, with large differences between studies (Reilly 2024). Daily symptom ratings over at least two symptomatic cycles establish the cyclical pattern and support confirming the diagnosis (Eisenlohr-Moul 2017); a clinician can make a provisional assessment and offer care before two cycles are complete.

What is AuDHD?

AuDHD is an informal, community term for being both autistic and having ADHD. It is not a separate diagnosis, but having both is common. Among autistic people in the studies pooled by two meta-analyses, about 28% had ADHD (Lai 2019) and about 38.5% currently had ADHD (Rong 2021); the figures differ because the studies and definitions differ. These describe co-occurrence in autistic people studied, not how common a separate “AuDHD” condition is.

Women are more likely to be missed. Screening studies suggest the true male-to-female ratio in autism is closer to 3:1 than the 4:1 seen in clinics, pointing to a diagnostic bias against girls (Loomes 2017).

Start with these

  1. Getting assessed as an adult woman

    How adult ADHD assessment works for women, why women are recognised late, and what screening tools can and cannot tell you.

  2. ADHD + PMDD: what the evidence says

    Women with ADHD screen positive for PMDD more often in several studies; how much more varies with the measure and sample, and we did not find a study confirming it with prospective daily ratings.

  3. Autism traits, sensory sensitivity & "AuDHD"

    ADHD and autism often co-occur; sensory sensitivity and camouflaging are not unique to autism; online screeners cannot confirm a diagnosis.

  4. PMDD treatments: what the evidence supports

    Treatments grouped into evidence tiers, anchored to the ACOG 2023 guideline and the 2024 Cochrane review. Strongest: SSRIs, with both continuous and luteal-phase dosing helping and continuous dosing favoured on average. Moderate: drospirenone pills and short-term GnRH agonists, with more trade-offs; a small 2026 network meta-analysis found SSRIs and pills both effective but cannot rank them. Limited: CBT, surgery, and calcium, B6 and vitex, which have some PMS evidence but insufficient evidence in confirmed PMDD. No eligible trial was found in women with ADHD or autism.

  5. Burnout recovery: what we know and don't

    What burnout means (occupational, autistic, "ADHD burnout"), its heavy overlap with depression, and contributors worth checking.

  6. Starting and adjusting ADHD medication as a woman

    Stimulants and atomoxetine are the medicines with consistent adult evidence, but Cochrane rates much of it low certainty and the trials skew male. Appetite, sleep and heart-rate effects are common and need monitoring. About half of adults stop within a year, and women are no more likely to stop. Evidence on sex differences and on premenstrual dosing is thin.

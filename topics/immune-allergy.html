Immune system & allergy

Allergy and autoimmune links are real but modest. Neuroinflammation is still a hypothesis.

What is solid

  • ADHD is associated with asthma and atopic disease after adjustment (Cortese 2018).
  • Some autoimmune links differ by sex; for example, inflammatory bowel disease is associated with ADHD in females (Hegvik 2018).

What is thin or missing

  • The inflammatory-marker meta-analysis is small and not robust (Misiak 2022).
  • No ADHD-specific peer-reviewed data exist for mast cell activation (MCAS).

How it connects

  • ADHD in women: Allergy and autoimmune associations, cause unknown Supported
  • Gut & pelvis: Immune-mediated gut conditions (IBD) linked in females Supported

Claims checked

Unsupported Preliminary

“ADHD is caused by (neuro)inflammation.”

A meta-analysis of only 10 studies found IL-6 modestly higher (g=0.71), but not robustly, TNF-alpha slightly lower and no difference in CRP and other markers (Misiak 2022). The only direct brain evidence is one PET study of 24 adults with ADHD vs 24 controls (Yokokura 2021), and an umbrella review rated peripheral biomarkers below the higher evidence classes (Kim 2020). Most human evidence for neuroinflammation as a risk factor is described as preliminary (Dunn 2019).

A more accurate way to say it: Some immune-related conditions are more common in ADHD, but there's no good evidence that inflammation causes ADHD.

Sources
  • Misiak 2022
  • Yokokura 2021
  • Kim 2020
  • Dunn 2019

Unsupported Popular, unsupported

“Histamine intolerance or 'high histamine' causes or worsens ADHD.”

Histamine-producing brain cells help keep us awake and attentive (Haas 2008), but no study has looked at histamine levels, histamine intolerance or antihistamine treatment in ADHD directly.

A more accurate way to say it: Histamine plays a role in alertness, but there's no research showing histamine intolerance causes or worsens ADHD.

Sources
  • Haas 2008

Unsupported Popular, unsupported

“MCAS (mast cell activation syndrome) drives ADHD and neurodivergence.”

There are no ADHD-specific peer-reviewed data on mast cells. The main source is a hypothesis paper about a subgroup of autism based largely on parent-reported 'allergic-like' symptoms (Theoharides 2012). Consensus criteria require typical symptoms, a documented rise in tryptase or other mast-cell mediators, and response to mast-cell treatment (Valent 2012).

A more accurate way to say it: There is no evidence that MCAS causes ADHD; MCAS is a specific diagnosis that needs lab-confirmed mediator rises, not just symptoms.

Sources
  • Theoharides 2012
  • Valent 2012

Partly true Supported

“Antihistamines make ADHD brain fog worse.”

First-generation (sedating) antihistamines impair attention, working memory and vigilance, with next-day carryover, while second-generation ones largely do not (Kay 2000). In a driving simulator, diphenhydramine 50 mg impaired driving more than alcohol at about 0.1% BAC, and people's own sense of drowsiness did not predict this (Weiler 2000). Antihistamines have not been studied in adults with ADHD; in children with eczema, prior antihistamine use was linked to more ADHD symptoms (OR 1.88; Schmitt 2018).

A more accurate way to say it: Older sedating antihistamines like diphenhydramine impair attention in everyone and may add to ADHD difficulties; newer non-drowsy ones generally don't.

Sources
  • Kay 2000
  • Weiler 2000
  • Schmitt 2018

Partly true Supported

“Research shows autism and ADHD express themselves in the body, through fibromyalgia, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, GI distress, POTS, migraines and chronic pain.”

She presents this as established by research, and as the basis of her 'gifted-neurodivergent' model. Associations are real for some conditions. People with EDS have higher rates of autism (RR 7.4) and ADHD (Cederlöf 2016). 51% of neurodivergent adults in one clinic sample had joint hypermobility vs 20% in the general population, and hypermobility mediated links to orthostatic intolerance and pain (Csecs 2022). Young autistic adults report more seizures, depression, hypertension and allergies (Fortuna 2015). However, a school sample found no link between hypermobility and neurodevelopmental problems (Glans 2025). The evidence shows co-occurrence, not that these conditions are expressions of neurodivergence.

A more accurate way to say it: Autism and ADHD co-occur more often than expected with some physical conditions, notably hypermobility/EDS and related pain and dizziness, but the reasons are not yet known.

Sources
  • Cederlöf 2016
  • Csecs 2022
  • Fortuna 2015
  • Glans 2025

Mostly true Established

“People with ADHD are more likely to have allergies, asthma and eczema.”

An umbrella review rated the link with childhood eczema (OR 1.31) as 'convincing' and with asthma (OR 1.51) as 'highly suggestive' (Kim 2020). The ADHD-asthma link held after adjusting for family factors (OR 1.45; Cortese 2018). Food allergy was not associated (OR 1.13, not significant; Miyazaki 2017), the associations are modest, and most data are from children.

A more accurate way to say it: Asthma and eczema are modestly more common in people with ADHD; this is a link, not evidence that allergies cause ADHD, and food allergy shows no link.

Sources
  • Kim 2020
  • Cortese 2018
  • Schans 2017
  • Miyazaki 2017

Key research

22 papers in this topic, strongest evidence first. Browse them all in the library.

  • Environmental risk factors, protective factors, and peripheral biomarkers for ADHD: an umbrella review Kim 2020, The Lancet Psychiatry Established
    A large review of reviews found that childhood eczema and asthma are among the most consistently reported factors linked to ADHD.
  • Association between attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and asthma: a systematic review and meta-analysis and a Swedish population-based study Cortese 2018, The Lancet Psychiatry Established
    Asthma and ADHD go together more often than chance, even after accounting for many other factors.
  • Definitions, Criteria and Global Classification of Mast Cell Disorders with Special Reference to Mast Cell Activation Syndromes: A Consensus Proposal Valent 2012, International Archives of Allergy and Immunology Established
    Experts set out how to diagnose mast cell activation syndrome, which requires lab evidence and not just symptoms.
  • Histamine in the Nervous System Haas 2008, Physiological Reviews Established
    The brain chemical histamine helps keep us awake and alert.
  • The effects of antihistamines on cognition and performance Kay 2000, Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology Established
    Older 'drowsy' antihistamines can dull attention and memory, sometimes into the next day, while newer non-drowsy ones mostly do not.
  • Association of Maternal Autoimmune Disease With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder in Children Nielsen 2021, JAMA Pediatrics Supported
    Children whose mothers have an autoimmune disease are slightly more likely to have ADHD.
  • Maternal immune activation and neuroinflammation in human neurodevelopmental disorders Han 2021, Nature Reviews Neurology Supported
    This review describes how a mother's immune system during pregnancy might influence brain development, mostly based on early and animal research.
  • The Relationship Between Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (hEDS), Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS), and Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS) Kucharik 2019, Clinical Reviews in Allergy & Immunology Supported
    A review found almost no solid research showing that hEDS, POTS and MCAS form a linked 'triad'; the idea rests mainly on overlapping symptoms.
  • Associations between attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and autoimmune diseases are modified by sex: a population-based cross-sectional study Hegvik 2018, European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry Supported
    ADHD was linked to some autoimmune conditions such as psoriasis, and some gut inflammatory diseases only in women.
  • Association of atopic diseases and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: A systematic review and meta-analyses Schans 2017, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews Supported
    Children with asthma, eczema or hay fever are somewhat more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD later.
  • Associations Between Autoimmune Diseases and Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Nationwide Study Nielsen 2017, Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry Supported
    People with an autoimmune disease, or whose mother has one, are a little more likely to have ADHD.
  • Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder in Offspring of Mothers With Inflammatory and Immune System Diseases Instanes 2017, Biological Psychiatry Supported
    Children of mothers with certain immune or inflammatory conditions were somewhat more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD.

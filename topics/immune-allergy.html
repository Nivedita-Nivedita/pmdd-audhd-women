Topics / Immune system & allergy

Immune system & allergy

Allergy and autoimmune links are real but modest. Neuroinflammation is still a hypothesis.

What is solid

  • ADHD is associated with asthma and atopic disease after adjustment (Cortese 2018).
  • Some autoimmune links differ by sex; for example, inflammatory bowel disease is associated with ADHD in females (Hegvik 2018).

What is thin or missing

  • The inflammatory-marker meta-analysis is small and not robust (Misiak 2022).
  • No ADHD-specific peer-reviewed data exist for mast cell activation (MCAS).

How it connects

  • ADHD in women: Allergy and autoimmune associations, cause unknown Supported
  • Gut & pelvis: Immune-mediated gut conditions (IBD) linked in females Supported

Claims checked

Untested   No direct evidence

Popular claim, paraphrased
Histamine intolerance or 'high histamine' causes or worsens ADHD.

Histamine-producing brain cells help keep us awake and attentive (Haas 2008), but we did not find a study in our search (last updated October 2026) of histamine levels, histamine intolerance or antihistamine treatment in ADHD directly.

Closer to the evidence: Histamine plays a role in alertness, but there's no research showing histamine intolerance causes or worsens ADHD.

Sources
  • Haas 2008

Untested   No direct evidence

Popular claim, paraphrased
MCAS (mast cell activation syndrome) drives ADHD and neurodivergence.

We did not find an ADHD-specific peer-reviewed study of mast cells or consensus-defined MCAS that met our selection rules (search last updated October 2026). The main source is a hypothesis paper about a subgroup of autism based largely on parent-reported 'allergic-like' symptoms (Theoharides 2012). MCAS itself is a defined diagnosis: consensus criteria require typical recurrent symptoms involving more than one organ system, a documented rise in tryptase or other mast-cell mediators above the person's baseline, and response to mast-cell treatment (Valent 2012; Valent 2020).

Closer to the evidence: MCAS is a real diagnosis that needs lab-confirmed mediator rises, not just symptoms, but there is no evidence that MCAS causes ADHD.

Sources
  • Theoharides 2012
  • Valent 2012
  • Valent 2020

Partly true   Supported

Popular claim, paraphrased
Antihistamines make ADHD brain fog worse.

First-generation (sedating) antihistamines impair attention, working memory and vigilance, with next-day carryover, while second-generation ones largely do not (Kay 2000). In a driving simulator, diphenhydramine 50 mg impaired driving more than alcohol at about 0.1% BAC, and people's own sense of drowsiness did not predict this (Weiler 2000). Antihistamines have not been studied in adults with ADHD; in children with eczema, prior antihistamine use was linked to more ADHD symptoms (OR 1.88; Schmitt 2018).

Closer to the evidence: Older sedating antihistamines like diphenhydramine impair attention in everyone and may add to ADHD difficulties; newer non-drowsy ones generally don't.

Sources
  • Kay 2000
  • Weiler 2000
  • Schmitt 2018

Partly true   Supported

Popular claim, paraphrased
Autism and ADHD show up throughout the body, for example through fibromyalgia, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, gut problems, POTS, migraines and chronic pain.

Associations are real for some of these conditions. In Swedish registers, people with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome had higher rates of autism (risk ratio 7.4) and ADHD (risk ratio 5.6) (Cederlöf 2016). In one clinic sample, 51% of 109 neurodivergent adults had generalised joint hypermobility vs 20% in the general population, and hypermobility mediated links to orthostatic intolerance and pain (Csecs 2022). Young autistic adults report more seizures, depression, hypertension and allergies (Fortuna 2015). However, a school sample of 11-year-olds found no clear association between hypermobility and neurodevelopmental problems (Glans 2025). The evidence shows co-occurrence, not that these conditions are expressions of neurodivergence or share one mechanism.

Closer to the evidence: Autism and ADHD co-occur more often than expected with some physical conditions, notably hypermobility/EDS and related pain and dizziness, but the reasons are not yet known.

Sources
  • Cederlöf 2016
  • Csecs 2022
  • Fortuna 2015
  • Glans 2025

Mostly true   Established

Popular claim, paraphrased
People with ADHD are more likely to have allergies, asthma and eczema.

An umbrella review rated the link with childhood eczema (OR 1.31) as 'convincing' and with asthma (OR 1.51) as 'highly suggestive' (Kim 2020). The ADHD-asthma link held after adjusting for family factors (OR 1.45; Cortese 2018). Food allergy was not associated (OR 1.13, not significant; Miyazaki 2017), the associations are modest, and most data are from children.

Closer to the evidence: Asthma and eczema are modestly more common in people with ADHD; this is a link, not evidence that allergies cause ADHD, and food allergy shows no link.

Sources
  • Kim 2020
  • Cortese 2018
  • Schans 2017
  • Miyazaki 2017

Key research

23 papers. A selection, listed by study design (reviews and trials first), then newest first. The label is the kind of study, not a quality score. See all in the library.

  • Diagnosis, Classification and Management of Mast Cell Activation Syndromes (MCAS) in the Era of Personalized Medicine

    Experts define MCAS as recurrent symptoms in more than one body system, plus a lab-measured rise in tryptase above your own baseline, plus a response to mast-cell treatment.

    Valent 2020, International Journal of Molecular Sciences · Consensus statement

  • Definitions, Criteria and Global Classification of Mast Cell Disorders with Special Reference to Mast Cell Activation Syndromes: A Consensus Proposal

    Experts set out how to diagnose mast cell activation syndrome, which requires lab evidence and not just symptoms.

    Valent 2012, International Archives of Allergy and Immunology · Consensus statement

  • Peripheral blood inflammatory markers in patients with attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD): A systematic review and meta-analysis

    Blood tests showed higher IL-6 and lower TNF-alpha in ADHD, but these results depended on single studies and need confirming in larger studies.

    Misiak 2022, Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology and Biological Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Association of Maternal Autoimmune Disease With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder in Children

    Children whose mothers have an autoimmune disease are slightly more likely to have ADHD.

    Nielsen 2021, JAMA Pediatrics · Meta-analysis

  • Environmental risk factors, protective factors, and peripheral biomarkers for ADHD: an umbrella review

    A large review of reviews found that childhood eczema and asthma are among the most consistently reported factors linked to ADHD.

    Kim 2020, The Lancet Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Association between attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and asthma: a systematic review and meta-analysis and a Swedish population-based study

    Asthma and ADHD go together more often than chance, even after accounting for many other factors.

    Cortese 2018, The Lancet Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Association of atopic diseases and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: A systematic review and meta-analyses

    Children with asthma, eczema or hay fever are somewhat more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD later.

    Schans 2017, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews · Meta-analysis

  • Allergic diseases in children with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Children with ADHD are more likely to have asthma and some other allergies, though the evidence is limited.

    Miyazaki 2017, BMC Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Prevalence of mast cell activation disorders and hereditary alpha tryptasemia among patients with postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome and Ehlers-Danlos syndrome: A systematic review

    A systematic review found no studies that properly tested whether mast cell disorders are more common in POTS or EDS.

    Farley 2025, Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology · Systematic review

  • The Relationship Between Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (hEDS), Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS), and Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS)

    A review found almost no solid research showing that hEDS, POTS and MCAS form a linked 'triad'; the idea rests mainly on overlapping symptoms.

    Kucharik 2019, Clinical Reviews in Allergy & Immunology · Systematic review

  • Effects of Fexofenadine, Diphenhydramine, and Alcohol on Driving Performance

    A common drowsy antihistamine affected simulated driving more than alcohol at about 0.1% blood alcohol, even when people did not feel sleepy.

    Weiler 2000, Annals of Internal Medicine · Randomised trial

  • Mast Cell Activation Disorder and Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome: A Clinical Association

    Some people with POTS who also had allergy-type, skin or gut symptoms had blood or urine results hinting at mast cell activation, but single abnormal tests do not prove it.

    Kohno 2021, Journal of the American Heart Association · Cohort study

Topics / Gut & pelvis

Gut & pelvis

Functional gut problems such as IBS are more common with ADHD; a link with endometriosis is preliminary.

What is solid

  • IBS and constipation are consistently more common in ADHD.

What is thin or missing

  • One Swedish register study links endometriosis and ADHD in both directions, including in sibling comparisons (Gao 2020); this is a single, preliminary finding.
  • Microbiome findings in ADHD vary widely and are not specific to ADHD.

How it connects

  • Iron & energy: Endometriosis and heavy bleeding drain iron stores Supported

Claims checked

Unsupported   Preliminary

Popular claim, paraphrased
ADHD is caused by gut bacteria, and probiotics can treat it.

Reviews describe ADHD microbiome findings as heterogeneous with no consistent differences (Bundgaard-Nielsen 2020; Gkougka 2022), and a multi-cohort adult analysis found large differences between cohorts (Jakobi 2024). Across psychiatric disorders, microbiome changes are mostly shared rather than specific to one disorder (Nikolova 2021). No probiotic trial in ADHD met the library's inclusion bar.

Closer to the evidence: Gut-brain research is interesting, but no consistent ADHD microbiome has been found and probiotics aren't a proven ADHD treatment.

Sources
  • Nikolova 2021
  • Jakobi 2024
  • Bundgaard-Nielsen 2020
  • Gkougka 2022

Partly true   Preliminary

Popular claim, paraphrased
Endometriosis and ADHD are linked.

A single Swedish national register cohort study, with a sibling comparison (173,650 families), found a two-way association: endometriosis was followed by a higher rate of ADHD diagnosis (sibling-adjusted hazard ratio up to 1.98), and earlier ADHD also predicted later endometriosis (Gao 2020). A register study shows association, not cause, and the finding has not been tested outside Sweden in our search. Separately, endometriosis was linked to depression (OR 3.61) and anxiety (OR 2.61) in UK Biobank, which did not study ADHD (Koller 2023). The mechanism is unknown.

Closer to the evidence: One large Swedish register study found endometriosis and ADHD occur together more than expected, in both directions; it's a single, preliminary finding about association, not cause.

Sources
  • Gao 2020
  • Koller 2023

Mostly true   Supported

Popular claim, paraphrased
ADHD and IBS go together.

A meta-analysis of 23 studies (over 1.9 million people) linked ADHD with IBS (OR 1.58) and constipation (OR 1.97), and constipation; studies of diagnosed organic gut disease were excluded, so that question wasn't tested (Blake 2026). In females, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis were also linked to ADHD in Norwegian registers (Hegvik 2018). Young adults with ADHD in an Israeli conscript cohort had more IBS (OR 1.67) but not IBD or coeliac disease (Kedem 2020). There is no meta-analysis specific to adult women.

Closer to the evidence: IBS and constipation are more common with ADHD; these are functional gut problems, and the link doesn't mean ADHD causes gut disease.

Sources
  • Blake 2026
  • Kedem 2020
  • McKeown 2013
  • Hegvik 2018

Mostly true   Supported

Popular claim, paraphrased
Endometriosis often gets misdiagnosed as IBS.

Women with endometriosis were 3.5 times more likely to have had an IBS diagnosis beforehand, and 2.5 times more likely to receive a new IBS diagnosis even after endometriosis was confirmed (Seaman 2008). In 171 women, median time from symptoms to diagnosis was 10.4 years and 74% received at least one false diagnosis (Hudelist 2012). The two conditions also genuinely overlap (summary RR 2.39; Saidi 2020), and the ESHRE guideline challenges laparoscopy as the only gold-standard test (Becker 2022).

Closer to the evidence: Endometriosis is often first labelled as IBS and takes years to diagnose; the two can also occur together, so ongoing pelvic and bowel pain deserves a gynaecological look.

Sources
  • Seaman 2008
  • Hudelist 2012
  • Saidi 2020
  • Chiaffarino 2021
  • Becker 2022

Key research

23 papers, strongest evidence first. See all in the library.

  • ESHRE guideline: endometriosis

    European specialists' guideline on how to diagnose and treat endometriosis, which no longer treats surgery as the only way to diagnose it.

    Becker 2022, Human Reproduction Open · Established

  • Endometriosis

    Endometriosis is common, affecting roughly 1 in 10 to 1 in 20 women of reproductive age.

    Zondervan 2018, Nature Reviews Disease Primers · Established

  • Epidemiologic and Genetic Associations of Endometriosis With Depression, Anxiety, and Eating Disorders

    Endometriosis was linked to depression, anxiety and eating disorders, partly through shared genes.

    Koller 2023, JAMA Network Open · Supported

  • Endometriosis and irritable bowel syndrome: similarities and differences in the spectrum of comorbidities

    Endometriosis and IBS often occur together but each has its own pattern of other health problems.

    Peters 2022, Human Reproduction · Supported

  • Endometriosis is a chronic systemic disease: clinical challenges and novel innovations

    A review describing endometriosis as a whole-body inflammatory condition, not only a pelvic one, that is often diagnosed late.

    Taylor 2021, The Lancet · Supported

  • Psychiatric comorbidity among women with endometriosis: nationwide cohort study in Sweden

    Women with endometriosis had higher risks of depression, anxiety and ADHD, and the links ran in both directions.

    Gao 2020, American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology · Supported

  • Clinical diagnosis of endometriosis: a call to action

    Experts argue endometriosis can often be diagnosed from symptoms and examination without waiting for surgery.

    Agarwal 2019, American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology · Supported

  • Association of Constipation and Fecal Incontinence With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

    Children with ADHD were more likely to have constipation and soiling.

    McKeown 2013, Pediatrics · Supported

  • Diagnostic delay for endometriosis in Austria and Germany: causes and possible consequences

    Women in Austria and Germany waited about 10 years for an endometriosis diagnosis, often after a misdiagnosis or after treating period pain as normal.

    Hudelist 2012, Human Reproduction · Supported

  • Impact of endometriosis on quality of life and work productivity: a multicenter study across ten countries

    Women with endometriosis waited an average of almost 7 years for diagnosis and lost about 11 hours of work productivity a week.

    Nnoaham 2011, Fertility and Sterility · Supported

  • Can symptomatology help in the diagnosis of endometriosis? Findings from a national case–control study—Part 1

    Pelvic pain, painful or heavy periods and IBS diagnoses were more common in women with endometriosis than in other women.

    Ballard 2008, BJOG: An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology · Supported

  • Endometriosis and its coexistence with irritable bowel syndrome and pelvic inflammatory disease: findings from a national case–control study—Part 2

    Women with endometriosis were more likely to have been diagnosed with IBS, sometimes perhaps wrongly.

    Seaman 2008, BJOG: An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology · Supported

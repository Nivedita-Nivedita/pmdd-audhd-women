Circadian rhythm & sleep

A late body clock is measurable in adult ADHD.

What is solid

  • Adults with ADHD and sleep-onset problems start releasing melatonin later in the evening (Van Veen 2010).

What is thin or missing

  • Melatonin shifts sleep timing. It does not treat attention.
  • 'ADHD is a circadian disorder' is a hypothesis about a subgroup.

How it connects

  • ADHD in women: Later melatonin onset in adult ADHD Supported

Claims checked

Unsupported Preliminary

“Morning sleep inertia is longer and more intense in ADHD and autism, so it can take hours to 'come online'.”

No study has measured sleep inertia in ADHD or autism. It is plausible: sleep inertia is worst when waking during the biological night (Trotti 2017), and adults with ADHD often have delayed circadian timing (Coogan 2017; Van Veen 2010). So waking at a conventional time may mean waking in one's biological night. That is an inference, not a finding.

A more accurate way to say it: Many people with ADHD have a later body clock, so early waking may hit during their biological night, when grogginess is worst; sleep inertia itself hasn't been measured in ADHD or autism.

Sources
  • Trotti 2017
  • Coogan 2017
  • Van Veen 2010

Unsupported Popular, unsupported

“Revenge bedtime procrastination is an ADHD thing.”

No peer-reviewed study has examined bedtime procrastination in ADHD. What is documented is a later chronotype and delayed melatonin onset and sleep onset in ADHD (Coogan 2017, 62 studies), and eveningness and longer sleep latency in adults with ADHD (Snitselaar 2017), which could make late bedtimes look like procrastination.

A more accurate way to say it: Going to bed late is common in ADHD and is partly explained by a delayed body clock; whether 'revenge' bedtime procrastination is specific to ADHD hasn't been studied.

Sources
  • Coogan 2017
  • Snitselaar 2017

Overstated Preliminary

“ADHD is really a circadian rhythm (body clock) disorder.”

Later chronotype and delayed melatonin onset are consistent findings in ADHD (62 studies; Coogan 2017), and 31 of 40 adults with ADHD had sleep-onset insomnia with a delayed melatonin rhythm (Van Veen 2010). But adult polysomnography shows no objective difference, even though self-reported sleep is worse (Diaz-Roman 2018). The idea that delayed rhythm causes ADHD symptoms applies, as a hypothesis, to a subgroup (Bijlenga 2019).

A more accurate way to say it: Many adults with ADHD have a delayed body clock that can worsen symptoms, but ADHD isn't simply a sleep disorder.

Sources
  • Coogan 2017
  • Van Veen 2010
  • Díaz-Román 2018
  • Bijlenga 2019

Overstated Supported

“Melatonin treats ADHD.”

In 105 children with ADHD and insomnia, melatonin advanced sleep onset by 26.9 minutes and increased sleep time but did not improve behaviour or cognition (Van der Heijden 2007). In 51 adults with ADHD and delayed sleep, 0.5 mg melatonin shifted the body clock by 1 h 28 min and reduced ADHD symptoms by 14%, but this disappeared 2 weeks after stopping (van Andel 2021), and without behavioural coaching actual sleep times did not shift (van Andel 2022).

A more accurate way to say it: Melatonin can help people with ADHD fall asleep earlier, but it is a sleep aid, not an ADHD treatment.

Sources
  • Van der Heijden 2007
  • van Andel 2021
  • van Andel 2022
  • Abdelgadir 2018

Key research

22 papers in this topic, strongest evidence first. Browse them all in the library.

  • Sleep in Children With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: Meta-Analysis of Subjective and Objective Studies Cortese 2009, Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry Established
    Children with ADHD have more trouble going to bed, falling asleep and staying awake in the day.
  • The sleep–circadian connection: pathways to understanding and supporting autistic children and adolescents and those with attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder Lok 2025, The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health Supported
    A review of how sleep and the body clock affect autistic and ADHD young people, and what treatments may help.
  • Sleep in adults with ADHD: Systematic review and meta-analysis of subjective and objective studies Díaz-Román 2018, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews Supported
    Adults with ADHD report worse sleep, though lab sleep studies do not always show differences.
  • Melatonin for the management of sleep problems in children with neurodevelopmental disorders: a systematic review and meta-analysis Abdelgadir 2018, Archives of Disease in Childhood Supported
    Melatonin helped children with neurodevelopmental conditions sleep longer and fall asleep faster.
  • Waking up is the hardest thing I do all day: Sleep inertia and sleep drunkenness Trotti 2017, Sleep Medicine Reviews Supported
    Grogginess after waking is a real, measurable state, worst when waking during your body's night. It is more severe in some sleep and mood disorders.
  • Stimulant Medications and Sleep for Youth With ADHD: A Meta-analysis Kidwell 2015, Pediatrics Supported
    Stimulant medicines can make it harder for children to fall asleep and shorten sleep, though the effect on sleep quality was smaller in children who had taken them longer.
  • Assessment and Management of Sleep Problems in Youths With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Cortese 2013, Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry Supported
    For children with ADHD, good sleep habits and behavioural approaches come first, with melatonin as an option for falling asleep.
  • Delayed Circadian Rhythm in Adults with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and Chronic Sleep-Onset Insomnia Van Veen 2010, Biological Psychiatry Supported
    Adults with ADHD who had trouble falling asleep had a later-running body clock.
  • Effect of Melatonin on Sleep, Behavior, and Cognition in ADHD and Chronic Sleep-Onset Insomnia Van der Heijden 2007, Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry Supported
    Melatonin helped children with ADHD fall asleep earlier but did not change behaviour or thinking.
  • A systematic review of circadian function, chronotype and chronotherapy in attention deficit hyperactivity disorder Coogan 2017, ADHD Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity Disorders Supported
    People with ADHD tend to be 'night owls' with a later body clock.
  • Sleep and Circadian Rhythmicity in Adult ADHD and the Effect of Stimulants Snitselaar 2017, Journal of Attention Disorders Supported
    Adults with ADHD often fall asleep late; stimulants can push the body clock later while light therapy can help bring it earlier.
  • Adult Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and Insomnia: an Update of the Literature Wynchank 2017, Current Psychiatry Reports Supported
    Insomnia is very common in adults with ADHD, and some ADHD medicines can make it worse.

Topics / Circadian rhythm & sleep

Circadian rhythm & sleep

A late body clock is measurable in adult ADHD.

What is solid

  • Adults with ADHD and sleep-onset problems start releasing melatonin later in the evening (Van Veen 2010).

What is thin or missing

  • Melatonin shifts sleep timing. It does not treat attention.
  • 'ADHD is a circadian disorder' is a hypothesis about a subgroup.

Claims checked

Untested   Preliminary

Popular claim, paraphrased
Morning grogginess (sleep inertia) is longer and stronger in ADHD and autism, so it can take hours to 'come online'.

We did not find a study measuring sleep inertia in ADHD or autism (search last updated October 2026), so the claim is untested rather than disproven. It is plausible: sleep inertia is worst when waking during the biological night (Trotti 2017), and adults with ADHD often have delayed circadian timing (Coogan 2017; Van Veen 2010). Waking at a conventional time may therefore mean waking in one's biological night. That is an inference, not a finding.

Closer to the evidence: Many people with ADHD have a later body clock, so early waking may hit during their biological night, when grogginess is worst; sleep inertia itself hasn't been measured in ADHD or autism.

Sources
  • Trotti 2017
  • Coogan 2017
  • Van Veen 2010

Untested   No direct evidence

Popular claim, paraphrased
Revenge bedtime procrastination is an ADHD thing.

We did not find a peer-reviewed study in our search (last updated October 2026) examining bedtime procrastination in ADHD. What is documented is a later chronotype and delayed melatonin onset and sleep onset in ADHD (Coogan 2017, 62 studies), and eveningness and longer sleep latency in adults with ADHD (Snitselaar 2017), which could make late bedtimes look like procrastination.

Closer to the evidence: Going to bed late is common in ADHD and is partly explained by a delayed body clock; whether 'revenge' bedtime procrastination is specific to ADHD hasn't been studied.

Sources
  • Coogan 2017
  • Snitselaar 2017

Overstated   Preliminary

Popular claim, paraphrased
ADHD is really a circadian rhythm (body clock) disorder.

Later chronotype and delayed melatonin onset are consistent findings in ADHD (62 studies; Coogan 2017), and 31 of 40 adults with ADHD had sleep-onset insomnia with a delayed melatonin rhythm (Van Veen 2010). But adult polysomnography shows no objective difference, even though self-reported sleep is worse (Diaz-Roman 2018). The idea that delayed rhythm causes ADHD symptoms applies, as a hypothesis, to a subgroup (Bijlenga 2019).

Closer to the evidence: Many adults with ADHD have a delayed body clock that can worsen symptoms, but ADHD isn't simply a sleep disorder.

Sources
  • Coogan 2017
  • Van Veen 2010
  • Díaz-Román 2018
  • Bijlenga 2019

Overstated   Supported

Popular claim, paraphrased
Melatonin treats ADHD.

In 105 children with ADHD and insomnia, melatonin advanced sleep onset by 26.9 minutes and increased sleep time but did not improve behaviour or cognition (Van der Heijden 2007). In 51 adults with ADHD and delayed sleep, 0.5 mg melatonin shifted the body clock by 1 h 28 min and reduced ADHD symptoms by 14%, but this disappeared 2 weeks after stopping (van Andel 2021), and without behavioural coaching actual sleep times did not shift (van Andel 2022).

Closer to the evidence: Melatonin can help people with ADHD fall asleep earlier, but it is a sleep aid, not an ADHD treatment.

Sources
  • Van der Heijden 2007
  • van Andel 2021
  • van Andel 2022
  • Abdelgadir 2018

Key research

24 papers. A selection, listed by study design (reviews and trials first), then newest first. The label is the kind of study, not a quality score. See all in the library.

  • Assessment and Management of Sleep Problems in Youths With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

    For children with ADHD, good sleep habits and behavioural approaches come first, with melatonin as an option for falling asleep.

    Cortese 2013, Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry · Consensus statement

  • Sleep in adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis of subjective and objective studies

    Autistic adults took longer to fall asleep and woke more in the night, both by their own report and on sleep measurements.

    Morgan 2020, Sleep Medicine · Meta-analysis

  • Sleep in adults with autism spectrum disorder and attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis

    Autistic adults and adults with ADHD both tend to take longer to fall asleep, wake more and sleep less well.

    Lugo 2020, European Neuropsychopharmacology · Meta-analysis

  • Sleep in adults with ADHD: Systematic review and meta-analysis of subjective and objective studies

    Adults with ADHD report worse sleep, though lab sleep studies do not always show differences.

    Díaz-Román 2018, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews · Meta-analysis

  • Melatonin for the management of sleep problems in children with neurodevelopmental disorders: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Melatonin helped children with neurodevelopmental conditions sleep longer and fall asleep faster.

    Abdelgadir 2018, Archives of Disease in Childhood · Meta-analysis

  • Stimulant Medications and Sleep for Youth With ADHD: A Meta-analysis

    Stimulant medicines can make it harder for children to fall asleep and shorten sleep, though the effect on sleep quality was smaller in children who had taken them longer.

    Kidwell 2015, Pediatrics · Meta-analysis

  • Sleep in Children With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: Meta-Analysis of Subjective and Objective Studies

    Children with ADHD have more trouble going to bed, falling asleep and staying awake in the day.

    Cortese 2009, Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Understanding the Impact of Stimulants on Sleep in ADHD: Evidence from Systematic Assessment of Sleep in Adults

    In adults, the effect of stimulant medicines on sleep is mixed and sometimes sleep improves.

    Surman 2022, CNS Drugs · Systematic review

  • A systematic review of circadian function, chronotype and chronotherapy in attention deficit hyperactivity disorder

    People with ADHD tend to be 'night owls' with a later body clock.

    Coogan 2017, ADHD Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity Disorders · Systematic review

  • Sleep and Circadian Rhythmicity in Adult ADHD and the Effect of Stimulants

    Adults with ADHD often fall asleep late; stimulants can push the body clock later while light therapy can help bring it earlier.

    Snitselaar 2017, Journal of Attention Disorders · Systematic review

  • Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome in Adults: A Randomized Clinical Trial on the Effects of Chronotherapy on Sleep

    Melatonin and light shifted the body clock but did not, on their own, make adults with ADHD go to sleep earlier.

    van Andel 2022, Journal of Biological Rhythms · Randomised trial

  • Effects of chronotherapy on circadian rhythm and ADHD symptoms in adults with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and delayed sleep phase syndrome: a randomized clinical trial

    Low-dose melatonin shifted the body clock earlier and slightly eased ADHD symptoms in adults, but the effect wore off after stopping.

    van Andel 2021, Chronobiology International · Randomised trial

Life stages & populations

Pregnancy, new motherhood, menopause, adolescence, culture and gender all change how ADHD and autism show up and get recognised.

What is solid

  • ADHD medication in pregnancy is largely reassuring in large registry studies; modafinil is the exception (see G26).
  • A history of premenstrual disorder strongly predicts depression around pregnancy, and the link runs both ways (Yang 2024).
  • New ADHD diagnoses in mothers peak 2–5 years after childbirth in Danish registers.
  • ADHD is under-diagnosed in US Black, Hispanic and especially Asian-American children and adults.

What is thin or missing

  • Breastfeeding on stimulants has only case-level data.
  • Women over 60 are not reported separately in any study found.
  • Stigma and help-seeking in South Asian and Indian women are unstudied.
  • ADHD in trans people is barely studied.

How it connects

  • Hormones & PMDD: Premenstrual disorder history predicts perinatal depression Supported
  • ADHD in women: Diagnosis delayed in girls and peaks in mothers after childbirth Supported

Key research

88 papers in this topic, strongest evidence first. Browse them all in the library.

  • Genetics of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder Faraone 2018, Molecular Psychiatry Established
    ADHD is highly heritable, so when a child is diagnosed it is common for a parent to have it too.
  • Cognitive effects of estradiol after menopause Henderson 2016, Neurology Established
    Estrogen taken after menopause did not improve or worsen memory or thinking skills, whenever it was started.
  • Psychostimulant Continuation and Postpartum Mental Health in Pregnant People With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Xu 2026, Obstetrics & Gynecology Supported
    Continuing ADHD stimulants during pregnancy was not linked to worse mental health after birth. Overall, 16.2% had a new mood or anxiety diagnosis in the year after birth.
  • In utero exposure to methylphenidate, amphetamines and atomoxetine and offspring neurodevelopmental disorders – a population-based cohort study and meta-analysis Bang Madsen 2025, Molecular Psychiatry Supported
    A large Swedish study, combined with Danish data, found no higher rate of ADHD or autism in children exposed to ADHD medicines before birth.
  • Pregnancy Outcomes in Women Diagnosed With Attention‐Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Population‐Based Register Study Andersson 2025, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica Supported
    The higher early-birth rate in women with ADHD largely disappeared once other mental health conditions and smoking were accounted for.
  • Menopause hormone therapy and risk of mild cognitive impairment or dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis Melville 2025, The Lancet Healthy Longevity Supported
    Hormone therapy at menopause did not raise or lower the risk of dementia.
  • Prescription Stimulant Use During Pregnancy and Risk of Neurodevelopmental Disorders in Children Suarez 2024, JAMA Psychiatry Supported
    Children exposed to stimulants in the womb looked more likely to get ADHD or autism at first, but this mostly disappeared once the mother's own ADHD and other factors were taken into account.
  • Methylphenidate and Atomoxetine in Pregnancy and Possible Adverse Fetal Outcomes di Giacomo 2024, JAMA Network Open Supported
    Pooling ten studies, methylphenidate and atomoxetine in pregnancy were not linked to more birth defects or miscarriages.
  • The bidirectional association between premenstrual disorders and perinatal depression: A nationwide register-based study from Sweden Yang 2024, PLOS Medicine Supported
    Women who had a premenstrual disorder before pregnancy were much more likely to be diagnosed with depression around pregnancy, and the link also ran the other way.
  • In utero exposure to ADHD medication and long-term offspring outcomes Bang Madsen 2023, Molecular Psychiatry Supported
    Among Danish mothers with ADHD, children of those who kept taking medication in pregnancy did not have more developmental conditions than children of those who stopped.
  • Time after time: failure to identify and support females with ADHD – a Swedish population register study Skoglund 2023, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry Supported
    Girls and women with ADHD were diagnosed about 4 years later than boys and men, and had more other mental health problems first.
  • Adult Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and the Risk of Dementia Levine 2023, JAMA Network Open Supported
    Older people with an adult ADHD diagnosis were more likely to develop dementia later, though this does not prove ADHD causes it.

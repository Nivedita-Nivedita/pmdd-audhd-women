Topics / Emotion & rejection sensitivity

Emotion & rejection sensitivity

Emotional dysregulation is a core feature of adult ADHD. 'RSD' is not a validated construct.

What is solid

  • Adults with ADHD show much more emotional dysregulation than controls (Beheshti 2020 meta-analysis).
  • Emotional dysregulation is now treated as part of the ADHD picture (Shaw 2014).

What is thin or missing

  • Rejection sensitivity has not been studied in adult women with ADHD.
  • People-pleasing constructs (sociotropy, self-silencing, fear of negative evaluation) are unstudied in ADHD.

How it connects

  • ADHD in women: Emotional dysregulation is common and impairing in children and adults with ADHD Established
  • Hormones & PMDD: Mood can worsen before periods: PMDD, or premenstrual exacerbation of another condition Supported
  • Autism overlap, sensory & burnout: About half of autistic people studied had difficulty identifying their emotions (alexithymia) Supported

What the research says about common claims

Childhood neglect, including emotional neglect, is linked with higher rates of depression later in life.

Established evidence Several good studies or reviews agree.

Who was studied: Meta-analyses of 124 studies and 192 samples of maltreatment and later depression in general populations

Emotional neglect, even without abuse, is among the childhood experiences most strongly linked to depression. Whether capable children are more often overlooked is plausible, but we did not find a study testing it (search last updated October 2026).

Read the evidence

You may have heard

“Capable, low-maintenance children are easy to emotionally neglect, and this kind of neglect (an absence of attunement rather than a single event) has lasting effects in adulthood.”

The popular version goes further: the lasting effects are well supported, but the idea that capable children are especially likely to be neglected is untested.

What the studies found

The adult-outcome part is well supported. Neglect was associated with depression (odds ratio 2.11) and suicide attempts (odds ratio 1.95) in a meta-analysis of 124 studies (Norman 2012). Emotional neglect was among the forms of maltreatment most strongly linked to depression across 192 samples (Humphreys 2020). The idea that capable children are more often overlooked is a reasonable clinical observation, but we did not find a study that tested it in our search (last updated October 2026).

Link to this claim

Emotional dysregulation is common and impairing in children and adults with ADHD, though it is not an official diagnostic criterion.

Established evidence Several good studies or reviews agree.

Who was studied: Meta-analyses of case-control studies in children and adults with ADHD, and meta-analyses of adult medication RCTs

Large meta-analyses find much stronger emotional reactions and mood swings in both children and adults with ADHD. Experts have not agreed how to define this, and ADHD medication usually eases it less than it eases attention symptoms.

Read the evidence

You may have heard

“Big emotions and emotional dysregulation are part of ADHD.”

The popular version matches the evidence well, though emotional difficulties are not yet part of the formal diagnosis.

What the studies found

Across 13 studies (N=2,535), adults with ADHD had much higher emotion dysregulation than controls (g=1.17), strongest for emotional lability (Beheshti 2020); in 77 youth studies the effect for emotional reactivity was d=.95 (Graziano 2016). Emotional symptoms are common and impairing, but there is no consensus on how to define them and they are not an official diagnostic criterion (Faraone 2018). Medication eases emotional lability about half as much as core symptoms (SMD -0.41 vs -0.8; Moukhtarian 2017).

Link to this claim

Adults with ADHD tend to have lower self-esteem than adults without it, and anxiety is common alongside ADHD.

Supported evidence Replicated, with some limits.

Who was studied: Systematic review of 11 self-esteem studies in adults with ADHD and a meta-analysis of large studies; qualitative study of 162 adults

Some people with ADHD describe people-pleasing, perhaps after years of criticism or alongside anxiety. We did not find a study testing people-pleasing or similar traits in ADHD (search last updated October 2026).

Read the evidence

You may have heard

“People-pleasing (or 'fawning') is an ADHD trait.”

This has not been tested directly; the popular version turns related findings about criticism, self-esteem and anxiety into an ADHD trait.

What the studies found

We did not find a peer-reviewed study in our search (last updated October 2026) of people-pleasing-type constructs (sociotropy, self-silencing, fear of negative evaluation) in ADHD. What is documented is that anxiety is common with ADHD (pooled OR 5.0; Hartman 2023), that adults with ADHD often feel criticised (162 adults; Beaton 2022), and that self-esteem is lower in most controlled studies (Pedersen 2024).

Link to this claim

A leading model, based mainly on animal studies, proposes that chronic stress may dampen the brain's reward and motivation systems.

Preliminary evidence Early, small or indirect studies. Plausible, not settled.

Who was studied: One narrative review of mainly animal studies; no studies in neurodivergent or high-functioning people

Long-term stress may dampen motivation and the pull of rewards, but the idea that wanting is hit harder than enjoying was not verified in the studies we checked. Loss of interest is also a core sign of depression and worth getting checked.

Read the evidence

You may have heard

“Chronic stress disrupts dopamine and dampens 'wanting' (anticipatory pleasure) more than enjoyment itself, which is why high-functioning people can quietly lose desire.”

The popular version goes further: it treats a model mostly from animal research as a settled explanation and adds a wanting-versus-enjoying distinction we did not verify.

What the studies found

A leading model proposes that anhedonia (loss of pleasure or interest) arises from stress disrupting brain reward systems, based largely on animal evidence that stress impairs dopamine pathways involved in incentive motivation (Pizzagalli 2014). The more specific claim that wanting is affected more than enjoying goes beyond what that review's summary states, and we did not verify it against other studies. Loss of interest is also a core symptom of depression, so it is worth getting checked rather than explained away as stress.

Link to this claim

In a small study of people with generalised anxiety, about nine in ten recorded worries did not come true.

Preliminary evidence Early, small or indirect studies. Plausible, not settled.

Who was studied: 29 people with generalised anxiety disorder tracked for 30 days, plus meta-analyses of intolerance of uncertainty

Some worries do pick up real problems early, but in the small study we found most recorded worries did not come true. We did not find a study testing whether anxious gifted, autistic or ADHD adults forecast threats more accurately (search last updated October 2026).

Read the evidence

You may have heard

“What gets diagnosed as generalised anxiety in bright or neurodivergent people is often accurate early pattern recognition rather than excessive worry.”

This has been tested indirectly and the evidence runs the other way.

What the studies found

It is true that the criteria for generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) do not ask whether a worry turns out to be accurate. But the direct evidence runs the other way. In 29 people with GAD, 91.4% of recorded worries did not come true, and people who saw this most clearly improved most in treatment (LaFreniere 2020). Intolerance of uncertainty is linked with GAD, depression and OCD symptoms to a similar degree (Gentes 2011) and is linked to anxiety in autistic people about as strongly as in others (Jenkinson 2020). In UK Biobank, high intelligence went with less generalised anxiety, not more (Williams 2022). We did not find a study testing whether anxious gifted, autistic or ADHD adults forecast threats more accurately (search last updated October 2026).

Link to this claim

Rejection sensitivity is a measurable trait, and in a large study of German young people it was higher in those with more ADHD symptoms.

Preliminary evidence Early, small or indirect studies. Plausible, not settled.

Who was studied: 78 US college men and 1,235 German 10-19-year-olds in cross-sectional studies; qualitative study of 162 adults

Many people with ADHD describe intense pain around rejection and criticism, but studies are few and mixed: college men with ADHD were not more rejection-sensitive. We did not find a study testing 'RSD' as a distinct ADHD symptom (search last updated October 2026).

Read the evidence

You may have heard

“Rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD) is a core part of ADHD that almost everyone with ADHD has.”

The popular version goes further: it treats an untested label as a core, near-universal ADHD symptom.

What the studies found

We did not find peer-reviewed validation of 'rejection sensitive dysphoria', or a questionnaire of its own, in our search (last updated October 2026). Rejection sensitivity is a real, measurable trait (Downey 1996), but ADHD studies are few and mixed: 78 college men with ADHD did not report higher rejection sensitivity (Canu 2007), while among 1,235 German 10-19-year-olds more ADHD symptoms went with more rejection sensitivity (Bondu 2014). We did not find a study in adult women in our search (last updated October 2026).

Key research

32 papers. A selection, listed by study design (reviews and trials first), then newest first. The label is the kind of study, not a quality score. See all in the library.

  • Emotion dysregulation in adults with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: a meta-analysis

    Pooling 13 studies, adults with ADHD had much more trouble managing emotions than other adults, especially quick mood changes.

    Beheshti 2020, BMC Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Pharmacotherapy of emotional dysregulation in adults with ADHD: A systematic review and meta-analysis

    ADHD medicines had small-to-moderate effects on adults' emotional regulation, and may be less effective for emotions than for core ADHD symptoms.

    Lenzi 2018, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews · Meta-analysis

  • Effects of stimulants and atomoxetine on emotional lability in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis

    ADHD medicines eased mood swings in adults, but only about half as much as they eased core ADHD symptoms.

    Moukhtarian 2017, European Psychiatry · Meta-analysis

  • Attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder and children's emotion dysregulation: A meta-analysis

    Across 77 studies, children with ADHD had much bigger and harder-to-manage emotional reactions than other children.

    Graziano 2016, Clinical Psychology Review · Meta-analysis

  • A meta-analysis of the relation of intolerance of uncertainty to symptoms of generalized anxiety disorder, major depressive disorder, and obsessive-compulsive disorder

    People who find uncertainty hard to bear tend to have more symptoms of generalised anxiety, depression and OCD. The link was similar for all three, a little stronger for generalised anxiety.

    Gentes 2011, Clinical Psychology Review · Meta-analysis

  • Self-Esteem in Adults With ADHD Using the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale: A Systematic Review

    Most studies found adults with ADHD have lower self-esteem, and this was similar for women and men.

    Pedersen 2024, Journal of Attention Disorders · Systematic review

  • The lived experiences of adults with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: A rapid review of qualitative evidence

    Adults with ADHD describe experiences that overlap with other conditions, which can lead to being misdiagnosed or diagnosed late.

    Ginapp 2022, Frontiers in Psychiatry · Systematic review

  • The self-esteem of adults diagnosed with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD): a systematic review of the literature

    Adults with ADHD tend to have lower self-esteem, and therapy may help improve it to some extent, though the evidence is limited.

    Cook 2014, ADHD Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity Disorders · Systematic review

  • Everyday emotional experience of adults with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: evidence for reactive and endogenous emotional lability

    When men with ADHD tracked their moods several times a day, they felt more irritation and frustration, sometimes triggered by events and sometimes not.

    Skirrow 2014, Psychological Medicine · Case-control study

  • Emotional lability, comorbidity and impairment in adults with attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder

    In a small study of men, quick mood changes seemed to come from ADHD itself rather than other conditions and made daily life harder.

    Skirrow 2013, Journal of Affective Disorders · Case-control study

  • The Kind Nature Behind the Unsocial Semblance: ADHD and Justice Sensitivity-A Pilot Study

    In a pilot study, adults with ADHD, especially the inattentive type, were more bothered by unfairness than other adults.

    Schäfer 2012, Journal of Attention Disorders · Case-control study

  • The relationship between death anxiety and severity of mental illnesses

    In 200 people in treatment, those with more fear of death had more severe symptoms across many disorders. The study cannot show which causes which.

    Menzies 2019, British Journal of Clinical Psychology · Cross-sectional study

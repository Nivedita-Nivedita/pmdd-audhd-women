About

PMDD & AuDHD in Women brings together research on PMDD, ADHD, autism and the connected body in women: hormones, the gut, the immune system, sleep, sensory load, burnout and life stages.

Who compiles this site

This site is compiled by Nivedita, a mathematician with a deep interest in cognitive neuroscience and lived experience of the conditions it covers. Nivedita is not a clinician. No page has yet been reviewed by a clinician; we are looking for clinical reviewers, especially for medication, pregnancy, suicide-safety, eating-disorder and autonomic (POTS) content. If you can help, use Share with us.

How the content was made

AI tools (Claude, by Anthropic) were used to search the literature, check each paper's details against its DOI record, compare each summary with the paper's abstract, and draft the guides. Nivedita directs the work, chooses what is included and reviews what is published. An independent audit in October 2026 found errors and outdated sources; corrections are being made and logged at the end of each guide and in the change history.

How we describe evidence

We use two separate things, so that the kind of study is never mistaken for how much to trust a statement.

1. Study design (on papers)

Each paper in the library is labelled with what kind of study it is: consensus statement, guideline, meta-analysis, systematic review, randomised trial, cohort or registry study, case-control, cross-sectional, questionnaire validation, narrative review, laboratory study, or other (often qualitative). Design tells you what a study can and cannot show. It is not a quality score.

2. Confidence (on statements)

Each specific statement in a guide, claim or symptom list carries our confidence in it:

  • Established

    Several good studies or reviews agree, and the finding applies to the people it is said to apply to.

  • Supported

    Replicated, but with limits: size, populations, methods or consistency.

  • Preliminary

    Small, single or early studies, or evidence from a different population. Plausible, not settled.

  • No direct evidence

    No study has tested the statement directly.

Confidence depends on the question, not just the design: a meta-analysis of poor studies can give low confidence, and qualitative research can give high confidence about what people experience. Where a review reports its own certainty rating (for example GRADE in Cochrane reviews), we give that rating too. A guide's headline grade is the confidence of its weakest essential statement; individual sections can be stronger.

3. Claim verdicts

Claim check gives each popular claim a verdict on how it is usually said: true, mostly true, partly true, overstated, untested (no adequate study exists yet, so it is not disproven) or contradicted (it has been tested and the evidence runs against it).

Literature search: PubMed/Europe PMC and Crossref, in English, mainly 2000–2026, with priority to consensus statements, guidelines, systematic reviews and large cohorts. Searches were last updated in October 2026. When we say we found no study, it means none in our search, not that none exists.

How papers are checked

Each paper's details are matched to its DOI record in Crossref, and each summary is compared with the paper's abstract and conclusions, or its legally open full text when there is no abstract (614 of 616 papers; the rest are original questionnaire papers with no abstract). This confirms the summary matches what the abstract reports. It is not a full-text appraisal or a clinical review, and abstracts often omit eligibility, harms and subgroup details.

Beta

The guides have not yet been reviewed by clinicians. They are for education, not diagnosis or treatment. Talk to a qualified clinician before changing anything about your care.

Corrections

Science changes and mistakes happen. Each guide ends with a corrections log, and every change to the site is kept in its public change history. To report an error, use Share with us and choose "Correction".

Privacy

No cookies, no analytics, no trackers, and no third-party scripts or fonts. If you use the Share with us form, your message is delivered to Nivedita by Formspree; only what you type is sent. On the symptom pages, ticks are kept only while the page is open unless you turn on "Remember my ticks"; then they are saved in your browser (never sent) until you press "Delete my ticks". Your light or dark choice is also saved in your browser. Like any website host, GitHub Pages may keep standard server logs.

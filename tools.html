Assessment tools

Validated questionnaires used in research and clinics. They can help you prepare for an assessment, but none of them can diagnose you.

An adult ADHD or autism diagnosis needs a clinician, your childhood history and ideally someone who knew you then. PMDD needs at least two cycles of daily symptom ratings — see the guide Tracking your cycle with the DRSP.

ASRS v1.1 (WHO Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale)Current DSM-IV adult ADHD symptoms (inattention, hyperactivity/impulsivity)SR. 18-item symptom checklist of DSM-IV Criterion A symptoms; the screener is 6 of the 18 questions (Part A/Part B labels and ~5 min timing are from the form, not the paper)**Free.** The 6-item screener is free for clinical and non-clinical use, including commercial use, with attribution and no modification (NYU TOV licence page). Hosted at Harvard NCS ASRS page. Copyright WHO / NYU / Harvard. *The Harvard page returned 503 errors while we checked; its terms come from the NYU page and search snippets.*Kessler RC et al. (2005) *Psychol Med* 35:245–256. 10.1017/S0033291704002892Kessler 2005 (154 NCS-R respondents): the unweighted 6-question screener outperformed the full 18 questions (sensitivity 68.7% v. 56.3%; specificity 99.5% v. 98.3%; kappa 0.76 v. 0.58). Based on DSM-IV wording, which is weighted toward observable hyperactivity. In a sample with major depression, sensitivity was only about 60% ([PMC5946096](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5946096/)). Women who mask, and high-IQ adults who compensate, may under-endorse items. Screening only.
ASRS-5 (ASRS for DSM-5)DSM-5 adult ADHD screenSR. 6 items with integer-weighted scoring, selected from the 29-question ASRS (full text: "optimal integer scoring rules")**Free for academic/non-industry use** under an NYU use agreement (needs an institutional signatory; 1-year term). Commercial/industry use needs a **paid licence** (NYU TOV: ASRS DSM-5)Ustun B, Adler LA, Rudin C, et al. (2017) *JAMA Psychiatry* 74(5):520–527. 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0298Developed with machine-learning item selection in two US general-population survey subsamples (household n = 119; managed care n = 218) and confirmed in a clinical sample (n = 300). Weighted general-population sensitivity 91.4%, specificity 96.0%; clinical sample sensitivity 91.9%, specificity 74.0%. No validation specific to women, gifted adults or masking was found. Scoring is proprietary, so use the official form.
DIVA-5 (Diagnostic Interview for ADHD in Adults)All 18 DSM-5 criteria in childhood and adulthood, plus impairment in 5 domains (education/work, relationships, family, leisure, self-confidence)CA, semi-structured interview. A partner or family member is ideally present. About 1–1.5 h (duration UNVERIFIED)**Proprietary, low cost:** €10 per DIVA (PDF) from the DIVA Foundation. 30+ languages. Validation list: divacenter.eu/more-information/validation. DIVA-5-R is announced.DIVA 2.0 (DSM-IV predecessor): Ramos-Quiroga JA et al. (2019; online 2016) *J Atten Disord* 23(10):1126–1135. 10.1177/1087054716646451 (40 outpatients with ADHD; 100% diagnostic agreement with the CAADID). DIVA-5 validations exist in Korean, Italian and Farsi (Italian, PMC11816683). **No English-language DIVA-5 validation DOI was confirmed (UNVERIFIED).**A clinical gold-standard-style interview used across Europe and in many Canadian and UK clinics. It includes example behaviours in each domain, which helps people who mask. Bring this to assessment; it is not for self-use.
CAARS / CAARS 2 (Conners' Adult ADHD Rating Scales)ADHD symptoms plus related scales (Inattention/Executive Dysfunction, Hyperactivity, Impulsivity, Emotional Dysregulation, Negative Self-Concept, DSM scales). CAARS 2 adds response-style/validity indices and impairment itemsSR + OR. CAARS 2: full 97 items, short 55 (SR) / 52 (OR), ADHD Index 12 items (counts from MHS-derived summaries). Original CAARS: long 66 / short 26 / screening 30 (UNVERIFIED counts)**Proprietary.** Sold by MHS (Multi-Health Systems), qualified purchasers only, usually administered by a clinicianOriginal CAARS: Erhardt D, Epstein JN, Conners CK, et al. (1999) *J Atten Disord* 3(3):153–158. 10.1177/108705479900300304 (4 factors: Inattention/Cognitive Problems, Hyperactivity/Restlessness, Impulsivity/Emotional Lability, Problems with Self-Concept; α .86–.92; median test-retest .89; overall diagnostic efficiency 85%). CAARS 2 manual (MHS, 2023): no DOINorms are separated by sex and age, which helps women (from MHS materials, not the 1999 paper). CAARS 2 includes an Emotional Dysregulation scale and a validity/over-reporting check. Your clinician will likely use it.
BAARS-IV (Barkley Adult ADHD Rating Scale-IV)Current and childhood (retrospective) DSM-IV ADHD symptoms, impairment domains, plus a sluggish cognitive tempo (SCT/CDS) item setSR + OR (current and childhood forms). Long form 5–7 min; Quick Screen 3–5 min. 18 DSM items + 9 SCT items (SCT count UNVERIFIED)**Proprietary.** Book with reproducible forms, Guilford Press (ISBN 9781609182038). The buyer may photocopy the formsBarkley RA (2011) *BAARS-IV*. Guilford. No DOI (manual)Norms for ages 18–39, 40–59 and 60–89. Includes a childhood self-report form and informant forms.
BDEFS (Barkley Deficits in Executive Functioning Scale, adult)Everyday executive-function deficits: time management, organisation/problem solving, self-restraint, self-motivation, emotion regulationSR + OR. Long form 89 items; short form 20 items**Proprietary.** Guilford Press (sample excerpt)Barkley RA (2011) *BDEFS*. Guilford, no DOI. Independent validation: *Barkley Deficits in Executive Functioning Scale (BDEFS): Validation in a large multisite college sample*, PMC8010583 (DOI **UNVERIFIED**)Especially useful for high-IQ adults. Cognitive EF test scores can fall in the normal range in this group (Milioni 2017; Antshel 2010 found average-range scores despite deficits relative to high-IQ controls), while Brown 2009 found impairment when daily-life EF ratings were included. Includes a Self-Regulation of Emotion subscale.
WURS / WURS-25 (Wender Utah Rating Scale)Retrospective childhood ADHD symptoms ("As a child I was…")SR. 61 items; the 25 that best separated ADHD from normal adults form the WURS-25. Score 0–100 (25 items × 0–4; range not stated in the abstract)**Free in practice:** widely reproduced, and hosted by CADDRA as a WURS-25 PDF. "Public domain" status comes from secondary sites only (**UNVERIFIED** from developer)Ward MF, Wender PH, Reimherr FW (1993) *Am J Psychiatry* 150(6):885–890. 10.1176/ajp.150.6.885 (Crossref lists the authors in a different order; PubMed's first author is Ward)Development sample: 81 adult ADHD outpatients, 100 normal adults, 70 adults with unipolar depression. A cut-off of 46 correctly identified 86% of ADHD patients, 99% of normal adults and 81% of depressed adults, so some depressed patients also score above it; it is not fully specific to ADHD. The abstract does not report sex; "developed in men" comes from secondary sources (UNVERIFIED). Items lean toward conduct and impulsivity ("temper", "trouble with police"; item content not checked against the full text), so inattentive girls may score low. Use alongside informant and school records.
AISRS (Adult ADHD Investigator Symptom Rating Scale)18 DSM ADHD symptoms with anchored prompts; mainly a treatment-outcome measure in trialsCA, semi-structured. 18 itemsCopyright held by investigators (Spencer/Adler, MGH/NYU). Access route **UNVERIFIED** (probably by request or licence via NYU)Spencer TJ, Adler LA, Qiao M, et al. (2010) *J Atten Disord* 14(1):57–68. 10.1177/1087054709347435. Expanded version: Silverstein MJ et al. (2019; online 2018) *J Atten Disord* 23(10):1101–1110. 10.1177/1087054718756198 (Crossref-confirmed)Validated with data from a 6-month placebo-controlled atomoxetine trial: high internal consistency, two subscales (hyperactivity-impulsivity, inattention), responsive to medication change. Mainly a research and clinical-trial tool. The expanded version adds executive-function-deficit and emotional-dyscontrol items (297 respondents, 171 with ADHD). You do not need this.
ACDS v1.2 (Adult ADHD Clinical Diagnostic Scale)Childhood and current DSM-IV ADHD symptoms plus expanded non-DSM adult symptoms (past 6 months and 14 non-DSM symptoms: full text)CA, semi-structuredAccess and licence **UNVERIFIED** (Adler & Spencer; not publicly listed)Kessler RC et al. (2010) Structure and diagnosis of adult ADHD: analysis of expanded symptom criteria from the ACDS. *Arch Gen Psychiatry* 67(11):1168–1178. 10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2010.146A research interview, rarely used clinically. In enriched community (n = 131) and health-plan (n = 214) samples, executive-functioning problems were the most consistent and discriminating predictors of DSM-IV adult ADHD, although they are not DSM criteria; inattention was less specific because it is strongly associated with other adult disorders. You do not need this.

Daydreaming, mind-wandering and cognitive disengagement (inattentive presentation)

InstrumentWhat it measuresFormatAccessKey paperNotes
MEWS (Mind Excessively Wandering Scale). The scale's actual name is "Excessively", not "Excessive"Excessive, hard-to-control, spontaneous mind-wandering ("thoughts on the go")SR. 12 items (full text), LikertItems are printed in the open-access validation paper (PMC6429624). Formal licence terms **UNVERIFIED**; it is presented as a free research/clinical toolMowlem FD, Skirrow C, Reid P, et al. (2019; online 2016) *J Atten Disord* 23(6):624–634. 10.1177/1087054716651927α > .9; sensitivity .9 and specificity .9 for the ADHD diagnosis, across two adult ADHD studies (full text: UK National Adult ADHD Clinic, SLaM/KCL; Study 1 was adult males only; optimal cut-off 15). Mind wandering contributed to impairment independently of core ADHD symptom dimensions, so it captures something DSM symptom counts miss. Sex-specific norms UNVERIFIED.
ACI (Adult Concentration Inventory)Cognitive disengagement syndrome (CDS, formerly sluggish cognitive tempo): daydreaming, mental fogginess, sluggishness, drowsinessSR. 16 items (full text; 0–3 scale, past six months), of which 10 showed strong convergent and discriminant validity from ADHD inattention and anxiety/depressionItems appear in the published paper. Licence **UNVERIFIED** (contact S.P. Becker)Becker SP, Burns GL, Garner AA, et al. (2018) *Psychol Assess* 30(3):296–310. 10.1037/pas0000476 (correction: 10.1037/pas0000517). Further validation: Fredrick JW et al. (2022) *Assessment* 29:1742–1755. 10.1177/10731911211027224First validated in a multi-university student sample (N = 3,172), which is young and educated. Fredrick 2022 replicated the 10-item SCT factor in a community sample (N = 286; mean age 44.45; 83.6% female), where SCT was uniquely associated with internalising symptoms, time-management and self-organisation difficulties, and poorer sleep. CDS can occur alongside ADHD or on its own (Barkley 2012).
Barkley SCT scale (adult)SCT/CDS symptomsSR. Embedded in BAARS-IV. The 2012 paper used a 9-symptom SCT set (threshold 5 of 9); that this is the BAARS-IV item set is UNVERIFIEDProprietary (part of BAARS-IV, Guilford)Barkley RA (2012) Distinguishing sluggish cognitive tempo from ADHD in adults. *J Abnorm Psychol* 121(4):978–990. 10.1037/a0023961Representative US sample of 1,249 adults aged 18–96. SCT formed a dimension distinct from ADHD, unrelated to sex; comorbidity with ADHD in about half of cases; a 5-of-9 threshold plus impairment would classify 5.1% of the population.
*Terminology*n/an/an/aBecker SP et al. (2023) Work group consensus renaming SCT to cognitive disengagement syndrome. *JAACAP* 62(6):629–645. 10.1016/j.jaac.2022.07.821The work group concludes CDS has reached recognition as a distinct syndrome, but its nature (separate disorder, transdiagnostic factor or specifier) is unresolved. CDS is not a DSM diagnosis.

Multi-section (exact item count UNVERIFIED)Published with the paper. Licence **UNVERIFIED**Hupfeld KE, Abagis TR, Shah P (2019; online 2018) Living "in the zone": hyperfocus in adult ADHD. *ADHD Atten Def Hyp Disord* 11:191–208. 10.1007/s12402-018-0272-y (a correction exists: PubMed 30848446)Pilot (n = 251) and replication (n = 372) samples of adults with or without ADHD; ADHD was indexed by symptom rating scales (including the CAARS), not clinical diagnosis. The newer, psychometrically validated version. Prefer it over the original AHQ.
Hyperfocus Scale (HFS)Hyperfocus plus related problems with time management and procrastinationSR. 11 items (count taken from the AHQ-D paper's description; UNVERIFIED from the primary source)Licence and access **UNVERIFIED** (developed in Turkey; English availability unclear)Ozel-Kizil ET, Kokurcan A, Aksoy UM, et al. (2016) Hyperfocusing as a dimension of adult ADHD. *Res Dev Disabil* 59:351–358. 10.1016/j.ridd.2016.09.016Validation: stimulant-naive (n = 53) and stimulant-treated (n = 79) adult ADHD patients both scored higher than university-student controls (n = 65), with no difference by stimulant use. Emotion dysregulation and rejection sensitivity

InstrumentWhat it measuresFormatAccessKey paperNotes
DERS (Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale)Emotion dysregulation: non-acceptance, goal-directed difficulties, impulse control, awareness, strategies, claritySR. 36 items (confirmed by the DERS-16 abstract), about 8 min**Free, not copyrighted** (per the NCTSN measure listing). Author contact: K.L. GratzGratz KL, Roemer L (2004) *J Psychopathol Behav Assess* 26(1):41–54. 10.1023/B:JOBA.0000007455.08539.94No abstract exists for Gratz & Roemer 2004 (Crossref, Europe PMC, PubMed, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex), so subscale names are not abstract-verified. Not specific to ADHD; elevated in BPD, PMDD, trauma and depression. Emotion dysregulation is not a DSM ADHD criterion, but it is common in adult ADHD. Consider rating it in both menstrual phases.
DERS-16Same constructs, brief version (no awareness subscale: full text)SR. 16 itemsFree (items in the paper)Bjureberg J, Ljótsson B, Tull MT, et al. (2016; online 2015) *J Psychopathol Behav Assess* 38:284–296. 10.1007/s10862-015-9514-xValidated in a clinical sample (N = 96) and two community samples (N = 102, 482): excellent internal consistency, good test-retest reliability, minimal loss of validity versus the 36-item DERS. Its brevity suits repeated tracking, e.g. follicular vs luteal phase (our suggestion; cycle-phase use was not tested).
A-RSQ (Adult Rejection Sensitivity Questionnaire)Anxious expectation of rejection (concern × expectancy)SR. 9 scenarios × 2 ratings; score = mean of 9 products (full text; the cited abstract describes only the attention studies)**Free** download from the Columbia Social Relations Lab measures page (the page refused our fetch with 403; availability is confirmed only via search snippets). Scoring sheet: Berenson, GettysburgBerenson KR, Gyurak A, Ayduk Ö, et al. (2009) *J Res Pers* 43(6):1064–1072. 10.1016/j.jrp.2009.07.007Measures rejection sensitivity as a social-cognitive trait. It is not a measure of "rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD)". RSD is a clinical term without a validated psychometric instrument (none found). Internet norms (full text, n = 685): M = 8.6, SD = 3.6. The cited paper's abstract reports that rejection sensitivity predicted disruption of attention by social-threat cues and threat-avoidant attention, which was associated with borderline personality features.

Masking and camouflaging

InstrumentWhat it measuresFormatAccessKey paperNotes
CAT-Q (Camouflaging Autistic Traits Questionnaire)Social camouflaging: compensation, masking, assimilationSR. 25 items, three factors; 1–7 Likert (full text), so totals range 25–175Items are in the published paper (author PDF via Autism Research Centre). Free online version at Embrace Autism (third party). Formal licence **UNVERIFIED**Hull L, Mandy W, Lai M-C, et al. (2019; online 2018) *J Autism Dev Disord* 49(3):819–833. 10.1007/s10803-018-3792-6Development: 354 autistic and 478 non-autistic adults online; α = 0.94, preliminary test-retest r = 0.77; equivalent factor structure across gender and diagnostic group. Validated for autistic traits, not ADHD. Adults with ADHD score above neurotypical adults but below autistic adults (van der Putten 2024, *Autism Res*, 10.1002/aur.3099, not the Hull paper). Use it descriptively. It is also relevant if autism is part of the differential.
ADHD-adapted CAT-Q items (research only)Social camouflaging of ADHD traitsSR, a novel item set "inspired by" the CAT-QNot publicly available as a validated toolWicherkiewicz F, Gambin M (2026; online 2024) Relations between social camouflaging, life satisfaction, and depression among Polish women with ADHD. *J Autism Dev Disord* 56(7):2875–2883. 10.1007/s10803-024-06410-6n = 329 women with self-reported ADHD diagnosis, online cross-sectional survey. More camouflaging went with lower life satisfaction and more depressive symptoms, after controlling for demographics. Not a validated instrument.
AAMM (Adult ADHD Masking Measure)ADHD masking/compensationSRBeta, from a private consultancy (bhcsmt.com/aamm)None. **No peer-reviewed validation**Do not rely on it.
*Status*No validated ADHD-specific masking or camouflaging measure was found as of Oct 2026. A *Br J Psychiatry* commentary, "Camouflaging in ADHD: the need for construct validation before clinical adoption", argues the construct does not yet transfer cleanly from autism ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/camouflaging-in-adhd-the-need-for-construct-validation-before-clinical-adoption/C318D0352CA1E95BCBBC695175EE03D1); authors and DOI UNVERIFIED).

PMDD and premenstrual disorders

InstrumentWhat it measuresFormatAccessKey paperNotes
DRSP (Daily Record of Severity of Problems)Daily ratings of DSM PMDD symptoms plus functional impairmentSR, prospective daily, 1–6 scale. 21 symptom items + 3 impairment items (scale and counts UNVERIFIED: not in the primary-source abstract)Copyright Endicott & Harrison (Columbia). Licensing information via Mapi eProvide. Widely reproduced for clinical use. IAPMD offers a free DRSP-based **PMDs Symptom Tracker** (iapmd.org/drsp; tracker)Endicott J, Nee J, Harrison W (2006; online 2005) *Arch Womens Ment Health* 9(1):41–49. 10.1007/s00737-005-0103-y (Study A n = 27; Study B n = 243 with PMDD; high test-retest reliability and internal consistency; sensitive to treatment change)IAPMD position ([Steps to Diagnosis](https://www.iapmd.org/steps-to-diagnosis)): daily DRSP tracking across at least 2 complete cycles is "the only recognized method" for an accurate diagnosis. IAPMD also offers a free PMDs Self-Screen. Track ADHD-relevant items (concentration, overwhelm) too. Note whether you are on stimulants or hormonal contraception.
C-PASS (Carolina Premenstrual Assessment Scoring System)Standardised DSM-5 PMDD diagnosis computed from DRSP ratings (PME identification is in UNC's C-PASS materials, not the 2017 paper: UNVERIFIED)Scoring system applied to 2+ cycles of daily ratings**Free.** Worksheet (PDF), Excel macro and SAS macro from the UNC Center for Women's Mood Disorders. No permission needed to use it unmodifiedEisenlohr-Moul TA, Girdler SS, Schmalenberger KM, et al. (2017) *Am J Psychiatry* 174(1):51–59. 10.1176/appi.ajp.2016.15121510200 women with 2–4 months of DRSP ratings; about 98% correct classification against expert clinicians; retrospective reports of premenstrual worsening were a poor predictor of prospective C-PASS diagnosis. In the 2017 paper, exacerbation of another disorder (DSM-5 Criterion E) was ruled out by SCID interview rather than by the scoring itself (full text), so separating PMDD from PME still needs a clinician. This matters with ADHD, because ADHD symptoms may worsen premenstrually without that being PMDD.
PSST (Premenstrual Symptoms Screening Tool)Retrospective screen for PMDD/severe PMSSR. 19 items (14 symptoms + 5 functional; counts not in the abstract, UNVERIFIED)**Copyrighted by McMaster University.** Licence by request to MILO (McMaster page). Free-use terms **UNVERIFIED**Steiner M, Macdougall M, Brown E (2003) *Arch Womens Ment Health* 6:203–209. 10.1007/s00737-003-0018-4Developed in 519 primary-care women aged 18–55; the authors report rates in line with prospective prevalence studies. It is retrospective, and retrospective reports were a poor predictor of prospective C-PASS diagnosis (Eisenlohr-Moul 2017). Screening only.

Circadian rhythm and chronotype (delayed body clock)

InstrumentWhat it measuresFormatAccessKey paperNotes
MCTQ (Munich ChronoType Questionnaire)Sleep timing on work and free days, light exposure and self-assessed chronotype (2003 paper). MSFsc and social jetlag were defined in later MCTQ papers, not the 2003 abstractSR, about 17 core questions (count UNVERIFIED). Variants: MCTQ^Shift, µMCTQFree (LMU Munich, Roenneberg). Official documentation at thewep.org/documentations/mctq (SSL error during our check; licence terms **UNVERIFIED**). R toolkit: rOpenSci mctqRoenneberg T, Wirz-Justice A, Merrow M (2003) *J Biol Rhythms* 18(1):80–90. 10.1177/07487304022396792003 pilot: 500 questionnaires; late chronotypes accumulated sleep debt on workdays and lengthened sleep on free days, and more outdoor light advanced sleep timing. Gives clock times rather than a preference score. Good for documenting delayed sleep phase and social jetlag. Shift work and alarm use on "free" days invalidate it (MCTQ documentation; not in the 2003 abstract).
MEQ (Horne–Östberg Morningness-Eveningness Questionnaire)Morningness/eveningness preferenceSR. 19 items (score 16–86)Free self-assessment version (MEQ-SA) and online AutoMEQ from the Center for Environmental TherapeuticsHorne JA, Östberg O (1976) *Int J Chronobiol* 4(2):97–110. **No DOI**; PMID 1027738 (from a secondary source; PubMed page not loaded)A preference trait; it does not diagnose delayed sleep–wake phase disorder (that needs sleep diaries or actigraphy, and ideally DLMO). CET maps scores to estimated melatonin timing for light therapy.

Interoception

InstrumentWhat it measuresFormatAccessKey paperNotes
MAIA-2 (Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness v2)8 dimensions: noticing, not-distracting, not-worrying, attention regulation, emotional awareness, self-regulation, body listening, trusting (names: full text)SR. 37 items**Free / public domain**, from the UCSF Osher Center (official)Mehling WE, Acree M, Stewart A, Silas J, Jones A (2018) *PLoS One* 13(12):e0208034. 10.1371/journal.pone.0208034Developed in 1,090 Science Museum London visitors; 8-factor model confirmed with improved internal consistency.

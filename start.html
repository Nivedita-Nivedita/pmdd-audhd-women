My cycle changes everything

  1. ADHD + PMDD: what the evidence says

    Women with ADHD screen positive for PMDD more often in several studies; how much more varies with the measure and sample, and no study has confirmed it with prospective daily ratings.

  2. Tracking your cycle with the DRSP

    How to rate symptoms daily with the DRSP for at least two cycles: the standard way to confirm a PMDD pattern. A clinician can assess you and offer care while you track.

  3. PMDD treatments ranked by evidence

    SSRIs have the strongest evidence; both continuous and luteal-phase dosing help, and the 2024 Cochrane review favours continuous dosing on average. Drospirenone pills offer modest benefit. GnRH agonists work but have significant effects. Surgery, CBT and supplements rest on small or PMS-only evidence, and nothing has been tested specifically in women with ADHD.

  4. Living with PMDD: relationships and getting taken seriously

    Self-selected surveys and interviews consistently describe years of dismissal and misdiagnosis, a 'dual identity' and strain on relationships. Daily ratings, the right clinician and partner-inclusive CBT are the practical levers. The evidence is consistent but mostly qualitative.

I'm exhausted or burnt out

  1. Burnout recovery: what we know and don't

    What burnout means (occupational, autistic, "ADHD burnout"), its heavy overlap with depression, and contributors worth checking.

  2. Talking to your GP about ferritin

    Iron deficiency without anaemia is common in menstruating women, and iron reduced fatigue in RCTs of women with ferritin of 50 or below.

  3. Night owl: delayed sleep timing in ADHD

    Many adults with ADHD have a delayed body clock; melatonin and light shift timing but are not ADHD treatments.

  4. Body signals: interoception, eating & daily function

    Interoception in ADHD is barely studied and findings conflict, while links between ADHD and eating disorders are well established.

My body: gut, allergies, POTS, pain

  1. Gut, pelvis & pain: IBS, endometriosis and ADHD

    ADHD is linked to functional gut problems such as IBS and constipation; endometriosis is often misdiagnosed as IBS; microbiome claims are preliminary.

  2. Immune, allergy & hypermobility: linked vs hype

    Atopy and autoimmunity are modestly but robustly linked to ADHD; hypermobility evidence is clinic-based; MCAS and neuroinflammation claims are unsupported.

  3. ADHD, autism and POTS

    What POTS is, why it mostly affects young women, how its brain fog differs from ADHD, what is and isn't known about links with autism, hypermobility, mast cells, the menstrual cycle and iron, and which treatments have evidence.

I'm starting or changing ADHD medication

  1. Starting and adjusting ADHD medication as a woman

    Stimulants and atomoxetine are the medicines with consistent adult evidence, but Cochrane rates much of it low certainty and the trials skew male. Appetite, sleep and heart-rate effects are common and need monitoring. About half of adults stop within a year, and women are no more likely to stop. Evidence on sex differences and on premenstrual dosing is thin.

  2. For clinicians: ADHD, the menstrual cycle & PME

  3. For clinicians: stimulants, heart rate and POTS

  4. ADHD medication in pregnancy and breastfeeding: deciding together

    What large registry studies and meta-analyses show about continuing or stopping ADHD medication in pregnancy, framed as a shared decision, with an honest note on how little breastfeeding data exist.
